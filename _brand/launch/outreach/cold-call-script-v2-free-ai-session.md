# Cold call script v2: free one-hour AI session for a testimonial

For the mortgage brokers in `2026-11-05-melbourne-weak-marketing-prospects.csv` (55 of them). A short tradie version is at the end. Bold lines are what you say. Everything else is for you.

---

## Read this once before you dial

**What you're offering:** one free hour, on a call or in person at their office. You ask a few questions about how they work, then show them three or four simple AI tricks they can use the same afternoon. No pitch in the session. All you ask in return is an honest testimonial if they found it useful.

**Why this is an easy yes.** Run it through the value equation:
- **Dream outcome:** an hour a day back on file notes, emails and document chasing.
- **Likelihood:** they watch it work on their own kind of task, in front of them.
- **Time delay:** they use it the same day.
- **Effort:** one hour, and you come to them.
- **Risk:** zero. It's free, there's no contract, and the testimonial is only if they liked it.

When the cost is one hour and the risk is zero, the only reason they say no is that they don't believe it'll be useful. So the whole call is about making it feel specific and useful *to them*.

**What you actually get out of it** (you don't say this part):
1. A real testimonial from a Melbourne broker. You have none right now, and it unlocks your ads page and pitch deck.
2. Your answers to "how do brokers actually work day to day", which makes every future sales call better.
3. A warm relationship. Some of these will buy the workshop ($997 to $4,500). Don't push it. Let them ask.

**Make the scarcity real.** The script says you're doing this with "a handful of Melbourne brokerages". Cap it (say 10 this month) so that's true, and say no once you hit it.

**Rules**
1. **Make it about them in the first 15 seconds.** Mention their firm, their suburb, their team size from the CSV.
2. **Ask before you tell.** Talk 30%, listen 70%.
3. **Be honest about the trade.** "I'm building up testimonials" is disarming. People trust a clear trade more than a free gift with no reason.
4. **Never promise compliance.** You're showing tips, not giving legal or compliance advice.
5. **Book it on the call.** Two time options, never "when suits?"

---

## 1. Gatekeeper

**"Hi, it's Sam from Locate Digital. Is [principal's name / the principal broker] around?"**

If they ask what it's about:
**"I'm running free one-hour AI sessions for a handful of Melbourne brokerages this month, showing the team a few shortcuts for file notes and admin. I wanted to see if [Business] would like one. Who'd be the right person for that?"**

If they're not in:
**"No worries. When's best to catch them, and is it this number or their mobile?"** Write the name and time in the CSV and call back exactly then.

*(Tip: the admin or office manager often feels the paperwork pain most. If they sound interested, say: "Honestly, you'd get the most out of it. Could you put it to [principal] for me?")*

---

## 2. The opener

**"Hi [name], it's Sam from Locate Digital. Have I caught you at an okay time for 30 seconds?"**

*(Pause. "What's it about?" counts as a yes.)*

**"I'll be straight with you. I help brokerages use AI properly, and I'm building up testimonials from Melbourne brokers. So I'm offering a handful of firms a free one-hour session. I come in, or we do it on a call, I ask a few questions about how your team works, and I show you three or four simple tricks for file notes, client emails and document chasing that you can use that afternoon.**
**No pitch. The only thing I ask is, if you found it useful, an honest testimonial at the end. Would that be useful for [Business]?"**

Then **stop talking.**

- **"Yeah, sounds good."** → skip to section 4 and book it.
- **"What kind of tricks?"** → section 3. Ask first, then give one example.
- **"We already use ChatGPT."** → section 5, that objection. It's actually your best lead.
- **"Not interested."** → section 5.

---

## 3. Discovery questions (2 or 3 is plenty)

These make the session feel built for them, and make them want it.

1. **"Is anyone in the team using ChatGPT or Copilot at the moment, even a little?"**
2. **"What eats the most time in a week? File notes, chasing documents, client emails, something else?"**
3. **"Roughly how long does a file note take after a client meeting?"**
4. **"Do you have any rules in the office about what can and can't go into AI tools?"** *(Most say no. Don't lecture. Just note it.)*

**Do the maths out loud:**
**"So if each broker spends [their answer, say 45 minutes] a day on notes and emails, and we cut even half of that, that's two or three hours a week each. With [team size] brokers, that's [hours] a week back. Would that be worth an hour of your time to see?"**

**Then give one quick example (pick the one that matches their pain):**
- File notes: **"For example, after a meeting you can turn your rough notes into a clean file note in about a minute, in your own format. You still check it, but you're not writing it from scratch."**
- Document chasing: **"For example, you can give it your fact-find checklist and it drafts the 'here's what we still need' email to the client, in your tone."**
- Client emails: **"For example, explaining a rate change or a lender policy in plain English to a nervous first home buyer. Two minutes instead of fifteen."**

---

## 4. Book it

**"Let's lock it in. Would you rather I come to you, or do it on a call?"**

Then two options:
**"Are you better Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon?"**

**"Perfect. Who else should be there? It works best with whoever does the most admin, plus any brokers who are keen."**

**"What's the best email for the invite? I'll send it through now."**

**One small ask to raise the show-up rate:**
**"Before then, could you think of one task that drives you nuts each week? I'll make sure we fix that one in the session."**
*(They've now invested in it, so they're far more likely to show up.)*

Log it in the CSV: `Outcome = Session booked`, date and time in `Follow-up`, plus their pain task in their words.

---

## 5. Objections

**"What's the catch?"**
**"Fair question. There isn't one beyond the testimonial. I'm new to doing this with brokers, and the hardest thing to get when you're starting is proof that it works. So I'd rather give a few firms something genuinely useful and earn the review. If it's not useful, you don't owe me anything, not even the review."**

**"We already use ChatGPT."**
**"Good, then you'll get even more out of it. Most teams I talk to are using it like a search engine. The jump is setting it up with your own templates and tone, so the output is usable straight away instead of needing a rewrite. Can I ask how you're using it now?"**

**"We're too busy."**
**"That's exactly why it's an hour. If it doesn't save you more than an hour in the first week, I've wasted your time. I can also do it before 9 or after 4 if that's easier."**

**"I'm worried about client privacy with AI."**
**"You should be, and honestly that's one of the most useful things we cover. A lot of brokers are pasting client details into free tools without realising where that data goes. I'll show you which kinds of tasks are fine, which ones to keep out, and how the business versions handle data differently."**
*(Stay general. You're pointing out risks, not giving compliance advice.)*

**"Send me some info."**
**"Happy to. Honestly, the info is the session itself. It's an hour, and it's free. Would Tuesday or Thursday be better? If neither works, I'll email you and you can pick a time."**

**"Is this a sales pitch for something bigger?"**
**"I do run a paid workshop and AI policy for brokerages, so I won't pretend it doesn't exist. But I won't bring it up in the session. If you ask about it at the end, I'll tell you. If you don't, you've just got some free tricks and I've got a testimonial."**

**"Not interested."**
**"No worries at all. Can I ask, is it that AI isn't on your radar yet, or that you've already got it sorted?"** *(If sorted:)* **"Love that. If you ever want a second set of eyes on your AI policy, give me a call."**

## 7. Running the hour (so you deliver and earn the testimonial)

| Time | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 min | Questions: tools they use, a normal day, the task they named on the call, any office rules on AI. Write their answers down, word for word. |
| 10 to 45 min | **Three or four tricks**, each on their real kind of task, using made-up or de-identified client details. Let them type, not you. |
| 45 to 55 min | **The privacy quick-check:** which tasks are fine, which data to keep out of free tools, why a business account matters. One page they keep. |
| 55 to 60 min | The testimonial ask, then you leave on time. Finishing early beats running over. |

**The tricks (pick the ones that match what they said):**
1. **Rough notes to file note:** paste dot points from a meeting, get a clean file note in their format. Always check it before saving.
2. **Missing-documents email:** give it the checklist and what's been received, get a polite chaser email in their tone.
3. **Plain-English explainer:** turn a rate change or lender policy update into a short client email.
4. **Reusable instructions:** save their tone, sign-off and templates once, so every answer comes out right the first time.
5. **Marketing in five minutes:** turn a de-identified settlement story into a Google Business post or newsletter paragraph.

**Ground rules to say out loud at the start:**
**"Two rules today: we only use made-up or de-identified client details, and anything AI writes, a human checks before it goes anywhere."**

---

## 8. The testimonial ask (the last five minutes)

Ask while the "wow" is still fresh. Don't wait for an email later.

**"Before I head off, was that useful?"** *(Wait. Let them say it in their own words.)*

If yes:
**"That means a lot. Would you mind giving me a short testimonial? It can be two or three sentences. To make it easy, can I ask three quick questions and write it up from your answers? I'll send it to you to approve before I use it anywhere."**

1. **"Before today, how were you using AI, if at all?"**
2. **"What's the one thing you'll use straight away?"**
3. **"Would you recommend this to another broker, and why?"**

Then:
**"Could I also use your name, firm and a photo? And would you be up for a 30-second video on your phone? Totally fine if not."**

Get it in writing: send the written-up version by email within 24 hours and ask them to reply "approved".

**Keep it honest.** Use their words, don't add numbers they didn't say, and make it clear (if asked) that the session was free. Testimonials have to be genuine under Australian Consumer Law, and it's a stronger story anyway.

**The soft next step (only if they ask):**
**"The full version is a half-day workshop for the whole team, plus a written AI use policy and check-ins at 30, 60 and 90 days. Happy to send you the details."**
Then stop. No pressure. A happy client who asks is worth ten who were pushed.

**Two days later:** text a thank-you with one extra tip they didn't get in the session. That's often when the referral comes: **"Do you know another broker who'd get value out of this?"**

---

## 9. Tradie version (same offer, different tricks)

Swap the opener's examples:
**"...and I show you three or four simple tricks for quotes, job follow-ups and Google reviews that you can use that afternoon."**

Tradie tricks: turn rough notes or a voice memo into a tidy quote, write the follow-up text after a quote, reply to Google reviews in a minute, and turn a job photo into a Facebook post.

Testimonial questions stay the same.

---

## 10. After every call

Fill in the CSV's last three columns:
- **Called?** Y
- **Outcome:** Session booked / Call back / Not now / Not a fit / No answer
- **Follow-up:** date and time, plus their biggest time-waster in their words. That's trick number one in the session.
