Sell first or buy first?

There's no single right answer. Here's how the two paths compare, before we look at your numbers.

Selling first

  • You know your budget

    The sale price is locked in before you buy, so there's no guessing.

  • You may need somewhere to stay

    If you haven't found the next place by settlement, you may rent for a while or negotiate a longer settlement.

Buying first, with bridging finance

  • You don't miss the right place

    You can buy when you find it, then sell without rushing.

  • You carry two loans for a while

    Interest builds up during the bridging period, and the lender sets a time limit to sell.

Next home questions

What people ask us most when they're moving on.

What is bridging finance?

A short-term loan that covers the gap when you buy your next home before your current one sells. It usually ends when your sale settles, and the lender sets a time limit to sell.

How much of my equity can I use?

It depends on your home's value, what you still owe and each lender's limits. We'll get an estimate of value and show you what's realistic.

Should I sell first or buy first?

Selling first gives you certainty about your budget. Buying first means you don't miss the right place. We'll walk through the costs and risks of both for your situation.

Can I stay with my current lender?

Sometimes that's simplest. We'll compare it with other lenders so you can see whether staying put still makes sense.
