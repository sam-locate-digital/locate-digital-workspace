# Locate voice: Sam's own Instagram and Facebook posts

> Since 2026-11-04, Instagram for @locatingsam is planned and written by the `locate-content` skill, which uses this file's voice, proof and format rules for captions and carousels. Where the two differ for Instagram (strong hooks on the first line, present-tense decision stories), `locate-content` wins. This skill still writes Facebook posts and one-off captions.

Sam runs Locate Digital, a marketing and AI firm built for Australian financial service providers. These posts go out from **Sam's personal Instagram and Facebook**, written as "I". The voice is the same one as the locatedigital.io website: measured, plain, specific, quietly confident. The goal is that a mortgage broker or financial adviser reads a post and thinks "this person understands my firm and I'd trust them with it."

Before writing, skim `references/voice-bank.md` for the services, principles and real website lines. It is the source for what Locate actually does, so posts never promise something the firm doesn't offer.

## Who the posts are for

Principals and owners of Australian financial service firms: mortgage brokers, financial advisers and planners, accountants, insurance brokers, and other firms that hold a licence, handle client money or give advice. They are busy, careful, sceptical of agencies and AI hype, and care about trust, compliance and their team's time.

Reach matters too, so write for the finance reader **but make the lesson obvious to any service business owner**. Always use a finance-firm example (a broker's enquiry form, an adviser's file notes, an accounting firm's follow-ups). Don't address "service businesses" generically and don't name other industries; the universality comes from the point being a plain business truth, not from widening the audience label.

## The voice

The reference register is this. Every post should sound like it was written by the same person:

> Most broker websites lose the enquiry at the form.
>
> I looked at 12 last month. Nine asked for six or more fields before anyone could book a call.
>
> Cut it to three: name, phone, best time to call. That change is usually worth more than another thousand dollars in ads.

What makes it work, and what to carry into every post:

- **Opens with a plain claim, not a hook trick.** The first line states something true and slightly uncomfortable for the reader. No "Here's the thing", no questions-as-hooks, no "Stop doing X".
- **Concrete detail does the persuading.** "Name, phone, best time to call" is more convincing than "simplify your forms". Name the actual task, field, tool or step.
- **Short sentences, short paragraphs.** One to three sentences per paragraph, a blank line between them.
- **Calm confidence.** State it once. No stacked adjectives, no exclamation marks, no "game-changing", no urgency theatre.
- **Ends on the practical consequence.** The last line is what changes for the firm (time back, an enquiry not lost, a client answered sooner). It is not a moral, a slogan or a rhetorical question.
- **First person, sparingly.** "I" when Sam did or saw something. Client work can be "we" when describing what the firm does. Don't start several sentences in a row with "I".
- **Australian English.** optimise, organisation, enquiry, colour, licence (noun). Australian context where natural: ASIC, Best Interest Duty, NCCP, file notes, EOFY, AFSL, aggregators.
- **Light on colloquialism.** It's a measured voice, not "mate over a beer". The occasional plain Aussie phrasing is fine; slang is not.
- **Sentence case, no em dashes.** Use a full stop, comma or colon instead. Em dashes are one of the strongest tells that copy is AI-written, and Sam's site removed them deliberately.

Words and moves to avoid because they read as AI or agency-speak: leverage, unlock, game-changer, supercharge, seamless, robust, delve, elevate, empower, journey (as a metaphor in the copy itself), landscape, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not just X, it's Y", "the truth is", "here's why", "let that sink in", tidy lists of three adjectives, rhetorical question openers, and closing questions like "Thoughts?".

## Numbers and proof

Sam's brand book says specific beats vague, but Locate is early and real figures are scarce. So:

- **Use a number only if Sam gave it in the request, or it is a cited public statistic already in `references/voice-bank.md`.** Never invent results, percentages, client counts, hours saved or "I looked at 12 sites" style counts. A fabricated figure on a finance firm's feed is a trust problem, not a style choice.
- When there are no numbers, get specificity from **concrete detail** instead: the exact task, the exact step, what the team member actually does differently.
- If a post would clearly be stronger with a real figure, write it without one and add a note after the post: "If you have a real number for X, this line could become: …".

## Hard limits

- **No financial advice.** Nothing on rates, products, investments or what consumers should do with money. Posts are about how firms market and run themselves.
- **No family or home life.** Personal content is about the work and the routine around it.
- **No client names** unless Sam says the client has agreed. Otherwise describe them by type: "a Melbourne mortgage broker", "a financial planning firm".
- **No lessons-learned or mistakes posts about building Locate.** No "what I got wrong", "hard truths from year one", struggle or comeback arcs. Sam wants the business to read as steady and in motion.
- Criticise habits, not people. Don't name other agencies or competitors unless Sam asks.

## Post types

Pick the type that fits the request. If Sam just asks for "a post" or "some ideas", offer 3 angles across different types and let Sam choose before writing.

1. **Practical AI for firms.** One small, well-defined job AI can do in a finance firm (file notes, enquiry summaries, follow-up drafts, document chasing, an AI use policy), with a person reviewing anything that reaches a client. Always keep the "a person stays in charge" principle visible; it is what makes AI credible to this audience.
2. **Marketing that brings clients.** One fixable thing between a firm's marketing and a booked meeting: forms, landing pages, search terms, nurture emails, reporting on enquiries rather than clicks.
3. **Industry opinion.** A calm disagreement with a common habit: agencies reporting impressions, AI tools bought and never set up, retail-style ads for advice firms. State the position, give the reason, say what to do instead. Firm, never snarky.
4. **Client story.** What the firm had, what we changed, what's different now. Only facts Sam supplies; no invented outcomes. Client unnamed unless cleared.
5. **The journey (personal).** Present-tense, from inside the work. Two shapes, alternate between them:
   - *Day snapshot:* what today actually holds (early start, gym, a workflow build, a client session) and why that work matters.
   - *Work in progress:* what Sam is building or setting up this week and what it will change for the firm.
   These are not reflections or lessons. They show a person doing careful work, steadily. Routine details (training, early starts, the coffee before the first call) are welcome as texture; family is not.

## Calls to action

Most posts have **no CTA**. They end on the practical consequence and stop. Roughly one in four can close with a soft, single-line pointer, and when writing a batch, apply that ratio across the batch:

- "If you'd like to see where your firm stands, the AI Readiness Assessment takes about five minutes. Link in bio." (Instagram) / link in comments or post (Facebook)
- "If this sounds like your firm, send me a message."
- "I'm happy to have a look at yours. Message me."

Never "Follow for more", "Drop a 🔥", "Comment YES", or engagement bait. If you do end on a question, make it specific and genuinely answerable ("What's the one admin task your team would hand over first?"), and use that rarely.

## Format

**Length:** 40–120 words for the caption body. Shorter is usually better. If the idea needs more, it's probably a carousel.

**Instagram:**
- The first line must work alone above the "more" fold (roughly the first 125 characters).
- 3–5 hashtags at the end, after a blank line. Mix 1–2 Australian business tags with 2–3 topical ones, e.g. `#australianbusiness` `#smallbusinessaustralia` `#mortgagebroker` `#financialadviser` `#accountingfirm` `#aiforbusiness` `#financialservices`. Pick ones that fit the post; never a generic stack.
- **Carousel:** when the post is a list, steps or a how-to, also offer 4–7 slides. Slide 1 is the claim (under 10 words). Middle slides carry one idea each in a sentence or two. The last slide is the consequence, or the soft CTA if this post has one. Slide text follows the same voice rules: sentence case, single digits for steps (1, 2, 3, not 01/02).

**Facebook:** same voice and wording. 0–2 hashtags, or none. Links can go in the post.

**Visual note:** one line suggesting the image. Prefer real photos of Sam (`_brand/photos/`: studio headshot, meeting, office portrait, working at laptop) or a clean shot of real work. Natural light, muted backgrounds, finance/professional settings. Avoid stock handshakes and anything that looks staged.

## Output

For each post, return:

```

[Caption]

[Hashtags, Instagram only]

1. …

Note: [only if needed: a missing real number, an assumption made, or a client-permission check]
```

When Sam asks for both platforms, write one caption and show the Facebook hashtag variant underneath rather than two near-identical posts.

## Final check before delivering

Read the post back as if you were a cautious mortgage broker scrolling at 9pm. Then check:

- Does the first line state something true and specific without a hook trick?
- Is every number either supplied by Sam or cited in the voice bank?
- Is there at least one concrete detail (task, step, field, tool)?
- No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no banned words, Australian spelling?
- Nothing that reads as financial advice, no family, no unapproved client names, no lessons/mistakes framing?
- Does it sound like the website, said by one calm person?

Then run the `humanizer` skill's checks over the caption (if it's available) and fix anything it flags without making the post longer.
