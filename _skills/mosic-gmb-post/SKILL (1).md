# MOSIC — Google My Business Post Generator

Generate two informational Google My Business posts for MOSIC per fortnight. Each post must be under 1,500 characters total. Warm, professional tone. Written for patients, not search engines.

## Why posts get taken down — read this first

Google removes GBP posts that read like ads or SEO copy. The most common triggers:
- Promotional language: "book now", "limited spots", "call today", "don't miss out"
- Keyword stuffing: cramming "osteopath Melbourne CBD" or location names into every sentence
- Offer/event framing: discounts, special prices, seasonal promotions
- Hard CTAs repeated throughout the post
- Language that reads like a Google Ads copy, not a health education piece

Posts that survive are purely educational — they look like something you'd pin on a clinic noticeboard. No agenda, no pitch. The clinic reference lives only in the closing line and the contact block.

## Post structure

Each post follows the style of the existing MOSIC GMB examples that have not been taken down:

1. **Title line with emoji** — short topic label, not a marketing headline. E.g. "Common Injuries for AFL Players 🏉"
2. **Opening sentence** — 1–2 sentences framing the topic and why it matters. Direct, factual.
3. **Subheadings with short bullet points** — 2–4 subheadings, 2–5 bullets each. Each bullet is a condition, symptom, or tip with a brief plain-English explanation. E.g. "Hamstring Strains: The most common AFL injury. Explosive sprinting places enormous load on the hamstring."
4. **Closing line** — one sentence that quietly connects the topic to osteopathic care. No "book now". E.g. "Regular osteopathic assessment identifies weaknesses before injury strikes." Or a soft question: "Returning with aches and pains? Book an appointment."
5. **Contact block** — always end with:
   📍 Melbourne Osteopathy Sports Injury Centre
   📞 (03) 9663 6202
   🌐 www.melbourneosteopathycentre.com.au

## Hard rules

- **Under 1,500 characters total including spaces** — count at the end and trim if needed
- Purely informational — no promotional language, no offers, no events, no discounts
- Educational tone — reads like patient advice pinned on a noticeboard, not a clinic ad
- Australian English throughout (practise not practice)
- Never mention competitors
- Clinic voice, not patient voice
- **No SEO keyword stuffing** — do not force "osteopath Melbourne CBD", "Bourke Street", or location signals into the post body. These are for website pages, not GBP posts. One incidental location reference is fine; more than that reads as SEO copy and risks takedown
- No repeated CTAs — one soft call to action at the close only
- Never frame Post 2 as a clinic pitch — no "what we do", "our approach", "we assess". The body must read as general health information a patient would find useful anywhere

## Two posts per fortnight

Each fortnightly run generates two GMB posts — one for each week. Both share the same broad topic as the blog post but take different angles:
- **Post 1:** the problem — what's causing it, who it affects, warning signs
- **Post 2:** management — general patient education on treatment approaches, self-care tips, and what to watch for. NOT "what MOSIC does". Write it as health information, not a service pitch. The only clinic reference is the closing line and contact block.

Both must be under 1,500 characters. Both go through the humanizer pass before saving.
