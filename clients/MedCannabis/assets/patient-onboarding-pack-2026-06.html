The basics

What medicinal cannabis is — and isn't

Medicinal cannabis refers to cannabis-based products that are prescribed by a doctor to manage specific medical conditions. In Australia, these products are approved and regulated by the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA).

There are two main active compounds in cannabis. CBD (cannabidiol) doesn't cause intoxication and is used mainly for anxiety, seizure disorders, and some pain conditions. THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) does affect cognition and mood, and is used for pain, sleep, appetite, and nausea — among other things. Most medicinal cannabis products contain some combination of the two, in varying ratios depending on the condition being treated.

These are not the same products available illicitly. Medicinal cannabis products are pharmaceutical-grade, with defined and tested concentrations. You know exactly what you're taking and how much.

Medicinal cannabis is not a cure for any condition. For most patients, it's a tool that helps manage symptoms — often when other treatments haven't worked well enough, or alongside them.

Conditions it's commonly used for

Chronic pain, anxiety, PTSD, sleep disorders, nausea from chemotherapy, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, fibromyalgia, and palliative care symptom management.

How it's taken

Oils (under the tongue), capsules, dried flower (vaporised, not smoked), and topical products. Your doctor will recommend the most appropriate form for your condition and circumstances.

What it won't do

Cure the underlying condition. Work identically for every patient. Be appropriate for everyone who asks. We assess every patient individually — not every referral results in a prescription.

Driving and medicinal cannabis

This is an important safety point. THC-containing products impair driving. You cannot drive while using THC, or for the period recommended by your prescribing doctor. CBD-only products are generally considered safe to drive on, but confirm with your doctor.

What is the TGA SAS-B pathway?

The Special Access Scheme (SAS) is how most patients access medicinal cannabis in Australia. Under SAS Category B, your doctor applies to the TGA for approval to prescribe a specific product for a specific patient. Approval is usually granted the same day. Your doctor handles the application — you just need to have the consultation.

What if I already have a prescription from another doctor?

You can bring an existing prescription to our dispensary and we'll fill it for you. You don't need to have a consultation here if your current prescriber is managing your treatment. If you'd like to transfer your care to us, we can arrange that — but there's no obligation.

Your first appointment

What to expect when you come in

Your initial consultation takes [30–45] minutes. It's a proper medical appointment, not a rubber stamp. The doctor will go through your medical history, your current symptoms, what you've already tried, and what you're hoping medicinal cannabis might help with.

Not every consultation results in a prescription. If the doctor decides cannabis isn't appropriate for your situation, they'll explain why and suggest what else might help. This doesn't mean you can never be prescribed — it might mean the timing isn't right, or a different approach is worth trying first.

Understanding your treatment

Your prescription and what it means

Medicinal cannabis prescriptions in Australia work similarly to other prescription medications. Your prescription covers a specific product, at a specific dose, for a defined period. You'll need a repeat prescription for ongoing supply — which is why regular follow-up appointments are part of the process.

Starting doses

Cannabis treatment almost always starts at a low dose, increased gradually over a few weeks. This is called titrating. It reduces the chance of side effects and helps identify the dose that works best for you.

When to expect results

For most patients, meaningful improvement in symptoms takes 2–6 weeks of consistent use. Don't adjust your dose in the first week based on not feeling much — allow your body time to respond.

Side effects

CBD-dominant products rarely cause side effects beyond mild drowsiness in some patients. THC-dominant products can cause sedation, increased appetite, dry mouth, and at higher doses, anxiety. Report anything unexpected to our clinic.

Storage

Store all cannabis products out of reach of children. Most products should be stored in a cool, dark place away from direct sunlight. Check the label — some products require refrigeration after opening.

Important safety information Medicinal cannabis is a prescription medicine. Do not share your prescription with others. Do not adjust your dose without consulting your prescribing doctor. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, tell your doctor before starting treatment. If you develop any unusual symptoms or mental health changes, contact us or your GP immediately.
Common questions

Questions we hear most often

Is medicinal cannabis legal in Australia?
Yes. Medicinal cannabis has been legally available in Australia since 2016, accessed through pathways regulated by the TGA. Products dispensed at a licensed clinic like ours are pharmaceutical-grade and legally prescribed. The product you receive is not the same as illicit cannabis.
Will I fail a roadside drug test?
This is one of the most important questions to raise with your prescribing doctor. THC-containing products can cause a positive result on roadside oral fluid tests, even after the therapeutic effect has worn off. CBD-only products are generally not detectable. If you drive for work or are concerned about drug testing, discuss this before starting any THC-containing treatment.
Can I use cannabis if I'm on other medications?
Possibly, but it needs to be assessed. Cannabis products interact with some medications — particularly blood thinners, some antidepressants, and sedatives. Bring a full medication list to your first appointment and your doctor will check for interactions before prescribing anything.
What does it cost?
Consultation fees are [PRICE] for an initial appointment. Medicare rebates apply to eligible in-person consultations, reducing your out-of-pocket cost. Product costs vary depending on the type prescribed — most patients spend between $150 and $350 per month on their prescription. Your pharmacist can walk you through the product costs before you commit to anything.
My GP doesn't know much about medicinal cannabis — is that a problem?
Not at all. We send your GP a clinical summary after every appointment so they're always up to date with your treatment. Many GPs prefer this arrangement — they stay informed without needing to manage the cannabis prescribing themselves. If your GP has questions, they can call our clinic directly.
What if it doesn't work for me?
That's a real possibility we're upfront about. Not every patient responds well to cannabis treatment. If the first product doesn't work, we'll review and try adjustments before concluding it's not appropriate for you. If it genuinely isn't helping after a proper trial period, we'll tell you honestly and help you think through alternatives.
Can I become dependent on medicinal cannabis?
CBD products do not carry dependence risk. THC-containing products carry a small dependence risk, comparable to some other prescription medicines. We screen for dependence risk factors before prescribing THC, and we monitor patients using THC-dominant products closely. If you have a personal or family history of substance use disorder, tell your doctor — it affects what can be safely prescribed.
