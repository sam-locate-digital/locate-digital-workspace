The constraint

No paid ads. That's actually good news.

Section 42DL of the Therapeutic Goods Act prohibits advertising Schedule 4 and Schedule 8 medicines directly to the general public. Google Ads and Meta both block cannabis advertising independently of the TGA. This clinic cannot run paid search or paid social campaigns for its core services.

Most clinics treat this as a problem. It isn't. It means every competitor faces the same restriction, and it levels the playing field between a new clinic and a 200,000-patient national telehealth giant. The only way to grow is organic — and nobody owns organic locally.

What competitors can't do

National telehealth players can't walk into local GP practices. They can't build individual referral relationships. They can't capture "near me" searches with genuine local authority. Their scale is a ceiling, not just a floor.

What this clinic can do that they can't

Build a GP referral network from a physical base. Own local search through GMB and suburb-specific SEO. Create clinical content that ranks for Melbourne-specific queries. Be the most trusted name in a 5 km radius.

Channel strategy

How each channel works

Google Business Profile
Critical — months 1–3

The single fastest path to local patients. "Medicinal cannabis clinic near me" and suburb-level searches surface the Google Maps pack first. A fully optimised GMB profile with genuine reviews is worth more in months 1–6 than any other channel.

  • Claim and verify the GBP listing
  • Complete every field: hours, services, photos, description, Q&A
  • Add the clinic as a "health and medical" category — specifically choose "alternative medicine practitioner" or "pharmacy"
  • Upload at least 10 professional photos (interior, exterior, consultation room, dispensary)
  • Add all services with descriptions
  • Write a 750-character business description that includes "Melbourne", your suburb, and key conditions — without sounding like keyword stuffing
  • Post 2–4 times per month (GMB posts are educational, never promotional)
  • Respond to every review within 48 hours
  • Request a review from every patient at their follow-up appointment (send SMS with direct Google review link)
  • Update holiday hours as relevant
  • Monitor and answer Q&A — pre-empt common patient questions
  • Target: 25 reviews by month 6, 50 by month 12
  • Send a review request SMS 24–48 hours after every appointment
  • Never incentivise reviews — it violates Google's policy
  • Respond to every review, positive and negative, personally
  • Product promotion (violates TGA advertising code)
  • Before/after patient testimonials referencing clinical outcomes
  • Discount or offer posts for prescription products
  • Any content that could be read as promoting cannabis use generally
SEO and content marketing
Critical — start month 1, compound over 12+ months

SEO takes time but builds a permanent asset. Every page that ranks is a source of patients at zero marginal cost. The strategy here is local first, then condition-specific, then educational.

  • Website live with proper meta tags, page speeds, and structured data (LocalBusiness schema)
  • Google Search Console and GA4 set up from day one
  • Location pages: Melbourne + up to 3 nearby suburbs
  • Services pages: one per main service (consultation, dispensary, telehealth)
  • Condition pages: one per condition pillar (chronic pain, anxiety/PTSD, neurological, palliative)
  • "medicinal cannabis clinic melbourne" — local authority
  • "cannabis dispensary melbourne [suburb]" — near-me intent
  • "medicinal cannabis for chronic pain melbourne" — condition + local
  • "independent cannabis clinic" — differentiation keyword
  • "how to get medical cannabis prescription australia" — educational funnel
  • Publish one blog post per fortnight minimum
  • All posts: educational, no promotional claims, 800–1200 words
  • Every post includes: "Medicinal cannabis is a prescription medicine. Speak to a doctor to find out if it's right for you."
  • Topics drawn from the content plan below
  • Get listed on healthdirect.gov.au and findmcprescriber.au
  • Submit to local Melbourne health directories
  • Guest articles in local GP newsletters or allied health publications
  • Partner with local patient advocacy groups for condition-specific conditions (e.g. chronic pain foundations)
Email marketing
High — ongoing retention from month 2

Email is the retention channel. You already have the patient's trust — email keeps them engaged, informed, and connected to the clinic between appointments. It also reactivates patients who've gone quiet.

  • Welcome sequence: 4 emails over 3 weeks for every new patient (what to expect, how to use their product, follow-up booking reminder, review request)
  • Monthly patient newsletter: one useful clinical update per month, soft booking CTA
  • Reactivation series: 3 emails for patients inactive 3+ months
  • Follow-up: 48-hour post-appointment check-in + review request
  • Only send to opted-in patients — consent at booking
  • Educational content only — no product promotions
  • Always include an unsubscribe link
  • Never make therapeutic claims ("this product will help your pain")
  • Never share patient list data with third parties
Organic social — Instagram and Facebook
Medium — brand building, patient education

Social builds ambient trust. People who follow the clinic before they become patients are more likely to book when the time comes. It's not a direct acquisition channel — think of it as a long-term credibility investment.

What to post (safe content)

  • Condition education: "What is neuropathic pain?" type posts
  • Clinic and team introduction posts
  • Process explainers: "How to access medicinal cannabis in Australia"
  • General wellness: sleep hygiene, stress management tips (no cannabis mention required)
  • Patient pathway infographics (what happens at your first appointment)
  • Destigmatisation posts: "Medicinal cannabis is a prescription medicine — here's what that means"

What to avoid

  • Images of cannabis products or the plant
  • Any claims about treating or curing conditions
  • Patient testimonials referencing outcomes
  • Any pricing or promotional posts
  • Content that glorifies or normalises recreational cannabis
  • Boosting posts that mention cannabis (Meta will restrict)
  • Posting before checking Meta's current Community Standards on regulated goods

Instagram strategy

  • Post 3–4 times per week in months 1–6
  • Mix: carousels (education), single images (clinic), Reels (short explainers)
  • Hashtags: health-adjacent, not cannabis-specific (#chronicpain, #sleepdisorder, #melbournehealth)
  • Stories daily if possible — behind the scenes, team, FAQs
  • Bio: "Melbourne's independent medicinal cannabis clinic + dispensary. Prescriptions by AHPRA-registered doctors."

Facebook strategy

  • Post 2–3 times per week — share blog content + original posts
  • Facebook Groups: join relevant Melbourne health groups — participate educationally, never promote
  • Create a Facebook Page (not a Group) for the clinic
  • Link GMB reviews to Facebook page for credibility
  • Do not run Facebook Ads — policy blocks cannabis-adjacent content

  • 2 blog posts published
  • Social: review which post types are getting engagement
  • Plan months 4–6 content calendar in detail
