Executive summary

The short version

The Melbourne medicinal cannabis market is growing fast, but the patient experience is poor. Two of the three dominant players are owned by cannabis suppliers, creating a structural conflict of interest that regulators, the media, and increasingly patients are noticing. Consultation quality has been called out publicly.

That creates a clear opening. A clinic that is genuinely independent, offers in-person care, and applies Medicare rebates sits in a category of one locally. This isn't a niche, it's an underserved segment of a 224,000-patient-approval market growing at 13% a year.

The biggest single opportunity: GP and specialist outreach. Only 52% of Australian GPs feel comfortable discussing medicinal cannabis with patients. An independent clinic that positions itself as a trusted referral destination, not a competitor, can build a referral pipeline that no telehealth-only service can replicate.

The sector is not slowing down. In the first half of 2025 alone, the TGA processed 113,000 SAS-B applications, a 27% increase on the same period the previous year. Flower-based products overtook oils as the most prescribed form. The top three conditions driving approvals are anxiety, chronic pain, and sleep disorders — all well within the patient profile this clinic will serve.

At the consolidation end of the market, Little Green Pharma and Cannatrek merged in 2026 to create a combined entity with approximately $112 million AUD in revenue. Vitura Health reported H1 FY2026 revenues of $67.9 million, up 8.3% on the prior period. The supply side is mature and scaling. On the patient access side, the gap between supply and quality clinical care is still wide.

Advertising constraint: Section 42DL of the Therapeutic Goods Act prohibits advertising Schedule 4 and Schedule 8 medicines to the general public. Google Ads and Meta both block cannabis advertising. The market is growing without paid acquisition, which means clinical reputation, referral networks, and organic search are the only real channels — and nobody is doing them particularly well.

Prioritised opportunities

Where to focus first

1
Own the "independent clinic" position before anyone else takes it
Evidence: CHOICE investigation has made supplier-clinic conflicts front of mind. Trueleaf owns this locally but has no digital strategy to capitalise on it. Alternaleaf and Polln cannot claim it at all.
Lead all marketing with independence and brand agnostic prescribing as the primary message, not cannabis treatment generally.
2
Build GP referral relationships as a primary acquisition channel
Evidence: 52% of GPs are uncomfortable discussing medicinal cannabis. No competitor offers structured GP education or referral pathways. This is a channel entirely open.
Launch a GP outreach programme in months 1–3. Host lunch-and-learn sessions within 5 km. Position as a clinical partner, not a competitor.
3
Create condition-specific pages for the four patient pillars
Evidence: chronic pain, anxiety, neurological, and oncology/palliative are underserved locally with quality clinical content. Telehealth players produce volume content, not depth.
Build four cornerstone pages — one per condition — with real clinical detail. These become the main SEO assets and GP information resources simultaneously.
4
Optimise GMB from week one — it's the most direct path to local patients
Evidence: "cannabis clinic near me" is a high-intent query dominated by Google Maps results. Trueleaf ranks locally but thin. A fully optimised profile with photos, posts, and early reviews can compete quickly.
Claim, verify, and fully optimise GMB profile before launch. Set up a review request process from day one. Post weekly in months 1–3.
5
Serve external script patients that telehealth clinics can't help
Evidence: Alternaleaf and Polln only dispense their own prescriptions. Patients with scripts from GPs or specialists have nowhere obvious to go in Melbourne for in-person dispensing.
Make external script dispensing explicit on the website and GMB. This is a largely invisible market segment. Be the first to name it clearly.
Strategic recommendation

The position to own

Melbourne's only genuinely independent medicinal cannabis clinic

The two dominant players in Australian medicinal cannabis are both owned by the companies whose products their doctors prescribe. That's not a conspiracy theory — it's a structural fact being reported by mainstream media. The market is ready for a clinic that doesn't have this problem, and says so clearly.

This clinic should lead with independence. Not cannabis broadly, not conditions, not telehealth access. Independence first, then patient care depth, then physical presence. That combination is unowned in Melbourne.

GP referral is the growth engine. Telehealth players cannot visit GP practices and cannot build clinical relationships with prescribers. An in-person clinic can. The 52% of GPs who don't feel comfortable discussing cannabis are not lost patients — they're future referral sources waiting to be educated.

Immediate (months 1–3)

GMB optimisation. GP outreach programme launch. Website live with condition pages and independence messaging. Review strategy in place.

Short term (months 3–6)

Condition-specific SEO content published. 20+ GP referral relationships active. Patient testimonial and review pipeline generating weekly reviews.

Medium term (months 6–12)

Ranking for 3–5 local high-intent keywords. 150+ active patients. Monthly GP newsletter or update running. External script dispensing explicitly marketed.

Year 2

Established referral network. SEO delivering consistent organic leads. Consider second location or expanded telehealth reach off the back of physical clinic reputation.
