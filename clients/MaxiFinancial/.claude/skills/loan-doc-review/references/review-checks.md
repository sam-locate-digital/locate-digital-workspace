# Consistency & compliance checks

Work through both sections for every file. The document-requirements reference tells you what should be
*present*; this reference tells you whether what's present actually *hangs together* and whether the
file holds the evidence responsible lending expects.

## Consistency reconciliations
Reconcile the same fact across different documents. Unexplained mismatches are where stale documents,
data-entry errors, and fraud surface — so an unexplained mismatch is at least 🟠 Review, and a mismatch
that a lender will reject on sight (identity, unverifiable income) is 🔴 Blocker.

**Identity & personal details**
- **Name** matches across photo ID, secondary ID, payslips, bank statements, and the application.
  Watch for maiden/married names, middle-name presence, and transposed given/family names. A different
  legal name with no supporting evidence (e.g. marriage certificate / change-of-name) is a Blocker.
- **Date of birth** matches across ID documents and the application.
- **Current residential address** matches across ID, statements and the application. A recent move can
  explain a mismatch — but then the file should evidence the current address.

**Income reconciliation (PAYG)**
- **Payslip net pay ↔ salary credits** in the bank statements: the net amount and pay frequency on the
  payslip should appear as credits, from an employer whose name matches the payslip. No matching salary
  credit for the stated employer is a serious flag.
- **Payslip YTD ↔ elapsed pay periods:** the YTD figure should be broadly consistent with the pay rate
  × periods elapsed in the financial year. A YTD that implies a very different annual figure than the
  income being relied on is worth a look.
- **Payslip income ↔ PAYG summary / tax return** for the prior year, where both are present.
- **Employer name** consistent between payslip, salary-credit narration, and any employment letter.

**Income reconciliation (self-employed)**
- **Tax return income ↔ Notice of Assessment:** the taxable income should match the NOA. A return with
  no matching NOA (or figures that don't match) is weak evidence.
- **Year-on-year trend:** compare the two years. A sharp drop in the latest year that the income
  relied upon ignores is a Review item (lenders often use the lower or an average). A sharp
  *increase* the file is relying on needs the latest financials/BAS to support it.
- **BAS turnover ↔ financials:** annualised BAS turnover should be in the same ballpark as the
  financials; a large divergence is worth flagging.

**Deposit / genuine savings**
- **Savings accumulation** in the transaction statements should show a genuine-savings pattern where
  the loan needs it. A large unexplained lump-sum deposit shortly before application, with no gift
  letter or documented source, is a Review-to-Blocker item depending on how much the deposit relies
  on it.

**Liabilities**
- **Declared liabilities ↔ statements & bank conduct:** repayments visible in the transaction
  statements (loan direct debits, card payments) should correspond to documented liabilities. A
  regular repayment leaving the account with no matching liability statement suggests an undisclosed
  debt — flag it.

## Compliance / Best Interests Duty checks
This is a **documentation-evidence check**, not a serviceability calculation or a suitability ruling.
You are confirming the file holds what a broker needs to demonstrate responsible lending and Best
Interests Duty under the NCCP Act. For each, mark whether the evidence is present, partial, or missing.

- **Income verified with evidence** — the income being relied on is backed by the documents in the
  file (not merely declared). Missing/unverifiable income for a relied-upon amount is a Blocker.
- **Living expenses verified against statements** — declared living expenses are supported by actual
  transaction-statement spending, not just a stated figure or a bare HEM benchmark. Declared expenses
  that look implausibly low against the visible spending is a classic responsible-lending gap → Review.
- **All liabilities captured and documented** — every debt with a statement, limits shown.
- **Objectives & requirements captured** — the file evidences why this loan / structure suits the
  client's stated objectives (fact-find / needs analysis present). Its absence undercuts BID.
- **Product recommendation supported** — where a recommendation is on file, there's evidence it was
  made in the client's best interests (e.g. comparison / reasoning), not just a single-product form.
  If no such evidence is in the folder, note it as a Review item — it may live elsewhere in the
  broker's system, so frame it as "confirm this is on file" rather than asserting it's missing.
- **Consents & disclosures** — privacy consent, credit-check authority, and credit guide/proposal
  disclosure evidenced.

## How to phrase compliance findings
Keep findings factual and evidence-based, and never cross into a credit decision:

- ✅ "Living expenses declared at $X; the 90-day statements show recurring spending nearer $Y — worth
  reconciling before lodgement." (states the evidence, asks for a human reconciliation)
- ✅ "No document evidencing the source of the $30k deposit credited on [date]; a gift letter or
  savings history is needed to support genuine savings."
- ❌ "The borrower can't afford this loan." (a suitability/credit judgment — not this skill's role)
- ❌ "This loan is approved / will be approved / the borrower qualifies." (never)

When a check depends on something that plausibly lives in the broker's CRM rather than the document
folder (fact-find, product comparison, signed disclosures), phrase it as "confirm this is on file"
and tier it 🟠 Review, rather than asserting it's missing — the folder is a subset of the full file.
