Where does it hurt?

Hyrox puts eight 1 km runs between eight strength stations, so the same joints and muscles get worked again and again. Find your sore spot below to see which stations usually aggravate it.

  • Knee

    Pain at the front of the knee, especially going down stairs or into a lunge.

    Usually from

    • Burpee broad jumps
    • Sandbag lunges
    • Wall balls

  • Lower back

    Stiffness or a sharp catch when you bend, push or carry.

    Usually from

    • Sled push
    • Sled pull
    • Row
    • Farmers carry

  • Achilles & calf

    Tightness or soreness above the heel, often worst first thing in the morning.

    Usually from

    • Running
    • Sled push

  • Shins & feet

    Shin splints along the inside of the shin, or plantar fasciitis under the heel.

    Usually from

    • Running

  • Shoulder

    An ache at the front or top of the shoulder when you throw, pull or press.

    Usually from

    • SkiErg
    • Burpee broad jumps
    • Row
    • Wall balls

  • Hip & hamstring

    Hip flexor strain at the front of the hip, or a pulling hamstring.

    Usually from

    • Running
    • Sled pull
    • Sandbag lunges

  • Neck, grip & wrist

    Tight traps and neck, sore forearms, or wrists that complain on the floor.

    Usually from

    • Sled pull
    • Farmers carry
    • Burpee broad jumps

Not listed? If it hurts when you train, we'll assess it.

Injured before Hyrox, with weeks to go?

It's the question we hear most: can I still race? For most training injuries, the answer is to keep training in a modified way rather than stopping completely.

The earlier you're assessed, the more options you have. A few weeks gives us time to settle the sore area, adjust your sessions and build strength before you ease off for race week.

Hyrox injury questions

Still unsure? Call us on (03) 9663 6202 and we'll point you in the right direction.

Should I keep training for Hyrox with an injury?

Often yes, in a modified way. Stopping completely can cost you fitness you don't need to lose. The aim is to find the movements that aggravate the injury, swap or scale them, and keep everything else going.

Sharp pain, swelling, or pain that gets worse each session is a sign to get assessed before you push on.

How close to race day is too late to get treated?

It's rarely too late to be assessed. Even in race week we can help you manage symptoms and plan how to approach each station.

The earlier you come in, though, the more options you have to treat, strengthen and adjust your training before you ease off for race week.

Do I need a GP referral?

No. You can book directly online or by phone. No referral is needed to see our osteopaths.

Can I claim on private health insurance?

Yes, depending on your fund and level of cover. Rebates vary between policies, so check with your insurer before your appointment.

Osteo or physio for a Hyrox injury?

Both osteopaths and physiotherapists are registered practitioners who treat sports injuries. If you searched for a Hyrox physio in Melbourne, our osteopaths offer the care you're after: assessment, hands-on treatment, exercise rehab and a plan for getting back to full training.

Our osteopaths specialise in sports and training injuries.

Do you do pre-race or post-race massage?

Yes. Our remedial massage therapists and myotherapists offer 30, 45 and 60 minute sessions. A lighter session in the days before the race can help you feel loose, and massage after Hyrox can help you recover.

Book ahead if you want a particular time during race week.

What should I bring or wear?

Wear clothes you can move in, such as shorts and a singlet or T-shirt. Bring the shoes you train in, any scans or reports about your injury, and a rough outline of your training week.

Bring your private health fund card if you'd like to claim.
