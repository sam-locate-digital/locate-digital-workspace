# MOSIC — Fortnightly Website Blog Post Generator

Generate a complete, publish-ready, SEO-optimised blog post for the MOSIC website. Target length: 1,200–1,500 words. Must target local Melbourne search intent, speak directly to male professionals aged 25–55, and beat competitor content on depth and local specificity.

**Clinic:** Melbourne Osteopathy Sports Injury Centre (MOSIC)
**Website:** melbourneosteopathycentre.com.au
**Locations:** Level 9, 140 Bourke St Melbourne CBD | 320–324 Keilor Rd Essendon North
**Phone:** 03 9663 6202
**Online booking:** Available
**Telehealth:** Also available
**Services to prioritise:** Osteopathy, running injuries, work injuries, corporate health, general health

## Target reader

Male professional, 25–55, based in or commuting to Melbourne CBD or Essendon. Time-poor and health-conscious but has a tendency to push through pain rather than address it. He plays sport on weekends (runs, gym, footy, golf), sits at a desk most of the day, and only searches for help when something is stopping him. He's smart — he doesn't need hand-holding, but he needs someone to cut through the noise and tell him what's actually going on and what to do about it.

City Osteopathy (cityosteopathy.com.au) posts 150–200 word articles with minimal local signals. Beat them on every dimension: depth, structure, local relevance, and practical value. A reader finishing a MOSIC blog post should feel genuinely informed — not like they just read a brochure.

### 2. Post body

**H1** — Close to the SEO title. Must include primary keyword and a Melbourne or location reference. This is what the reader sees first; make it earn attention.

**Introduction — 150–200 words**
Open with a relatable problem or a specific, surprising fact. Name the Melbourne context in the first two sentences — don't stay vague. The reader should feel like this was written for them, not for a generic Australian audience. No throat-clearing, no "in today's world" openers.

**H2: [Section 1 — The problem] — 200–250 words**
Explain the condition or issue in depth. Why does it happen? What causes it for a typical CBD professional or active Melbourne male? Be specific — name the patterns (sitting posture at a standing desk, impact from running on concrete paths, asymmetrical load from a bag on one shoulder). Plain language, no jargon without a plain-English explanation.

**H2: [Section 2 — How osteopathy helps] — 200–250 words**
What does osteopathic treatment actually do for this? What can the patient expect from a session at MOSIC? Ground this in MOSIC's approach — holistic, root cause focused, not just symptom management. Avoid vague claims; be specific about what practitioners assess and address.

**[Mid-post CTA — single line]**
Insert after Section 2: "If this sounds familiar, book online at melbourneosteopathycentre.com.au or call 03 9663 6202."

**H2: [Section 3 — What you can do] — 200–250 words**
Practical self-management tips the reader can act on today. Real value here builds trust and keeps time-on-page high. Can include specific exercises, posture adjustments, movement breaks, or lifestyle changes. Don't be generic — tailor to the topic and the Melbourne professional lifestyle.

**H2: [Section 4 — Local or seasonal angle] — 150–200 words**
Ground the topic in Melbourne context. E.g. "For those working near Bourke Street...", "With Melbourne's winter...", "As AFL season ramps up...", "During marathon training season...". This is where local search signals land most naturally. Skip this section if the topic is already strongly local.

**Conclusion + CTA — 100–150 words**
Briefly pull the key insight together (one or two sentences, not a full recap). Then prompt action with both channels on separate lines:
- Book online: melbourneosteopathycentre.com.au
- Call: 03 9663 6202 (Bourke St CBD or Essendon)
- Telehealth appointments also available

### 3. FAQ section (mandatory — add after the conclusion)

- Comparison: e.g. "What's the difference between an osteopath and a physio for [topic]?"
- Timeline: e.g. "How many sessions will I need for [topic]?"

- Admin/coverage: e.g. "Is [condition] covered by WorkCover / private health?"

[Answer — plain prose, 60–100 words. No AI vocabulary. Australian English.]

## Writing style rules

- Australian English throughout — practise, programme, honour, recognised
- Short paragraphs — 3 to 5 sentences max, then a line break
- Warm but authoritative — a knowledgeable practitioner talking to a smart adult, not a patient handout
- No jargon without a plain explanation immediately after
- Vary how you refer to the clinic — "our team", "MOSIC's practitioners", "the Bourke Street clinic", not just "MOSIC" every time
- Banned phrases: "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "holistic approach to wellness", "journey", "empower"
- Never AI-sounding — read it back and if it sounds like a press release, rewrite it
