# MOSIC brand voice — social carousels

Built from melbourneosteopathycentre.com.au (home page, the Melbourne Marathon recovery and spring running blogs) and past MOSIC carousels.

## Who we're talking to

Active Melbourne professional, 25–55, mostly men. Works at a desk in the CBD or lives around Essendon. Plays footy, runs the Tan, lifts, golfs. Pushes through pain until something stops him. Smart, time-poor, allergic to fluff. He'll swipe if slide 1 names *his* problem.

## How MOSIC sounds

- **Straight-talking clinician, not a brochure.** States the pattern plainly: "Most of these aren't accidents. They're predictable."
- **Second person, short sentences, the odd fragment.** "Keep moving, gently." "Don't wait for something to hurt."
- **"We" = the osteo team,** used for experience and approach: "Our team sees this pattern every year."
- **Root cause over symptoms.** The signature idea: treat what caused it, not just where it hurts. The whole body (kinetic chain) contributes.
- **Plain English first, then the term.** "Tissue tolerance", "load", "tendinopathy" are fine once explained.
- **Hedged on outcomes.** "Usually", "often", "for most people", "depends on". Never promise a timeline or result.
- **Specifically Melbourne.** The Tan, Albert Park Lake, the MCG, Princes Park, Bourke St and Collins St offices, Essendon, winter grounds, daylight saving, Run Melbourne, the Melbourne Marathon, AFL season. One local reference per carousel minimum, ideally on its own slide.
- **Soft CTAs.** "Worth getting looked at properly." "Book online or give us a call." No urgency tricks.
- Brand lines to reach for occasionally: "Sustained health", "Making Melbourne Australia's healthiest city".

## Words and habits to avoid

- AI vocabulary: unlock, elevate, journey, holistic wellness, game-changer, delve, empower, transform, "in today's fast-paced world".
- Em dashes (use a full stop or comma). Exclamation marks (one per carousel at most, ideally none).
- American spelling. Use Australian English: recognise, mobilise, programme is fine but "program" also OK, centre, physio.
- Emojis on slides. (One or two in the caption is fine.)

## AHPRA advertising rules (non-negotiable — MOSIC practitioners are AHPRA-registered)

- No testimonials or patient reviews on slides or in captions.
- No guaranteed outcomes ("fix your back in 3 sessions", "pain-free for good").
- No claims of cure, and no "best/leading/number one osteo in Melbourne".
- No invented statistics. Only use a number if it's well-established (e.g. hamstring reinjury risk is highest in the first weeks back) and phrase it cautiously, or leave it out.
- No fear-mongering. Concern is fine; scare tactics aren't.
- "Practitioner quote" slides must be MOSIC's own approach in general terms, not a patient story.
