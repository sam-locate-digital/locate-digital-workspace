## Caption

Most laptop neck pain isn't one bad day. It's the screen.

A laptop on a desk sits well below eye level, so your head drifts forward to meet it. Hold that for a full day on Collins St or at the kitchen table and the muscles at the back of your neck and across your shoulders end up doing overtime.

Two things that usually help:
Get the top of the screen to eye level. A stand and a separate keyboard, or a stack of books, both work.
Get up every 30 minutes. A walk to the kitchen counts.

If it's still grumbling after a week or two of that, it's worth getting looked at properly.
