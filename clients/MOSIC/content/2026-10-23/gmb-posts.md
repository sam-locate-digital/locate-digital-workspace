## Post 1 — the problem (week of 26 October)

Golf and lower back pain ⛳

With longer evenings, a lot of people are fitting in more golf after work. Lower back pain is one of the most common golf complaints, and it often starts away from the course.

Why the back takes the load
• The swing is a twist: power should come from the hips and upper back, with the lower back staying fairly steady.
• Long hours sitting: desk days tighten the front of the hips and stiffen the upper back, so they turn less.
• The lower back makes up the gap: when the hips and upper back can't rotate, the lower back twists more than it's built to.
• A quick ramp-up: going from a few rounds over winter to two or three a week gives the back little time to adjust.

Signs your back is struggling
• A dull ache on one side of the lower back after a round
• Stiffness the next morning that eases once you're moving
• A sharp catch at the top of the backswing or just after impact
• Soreness that builds on the drive home

Get it checked sooner if
• Pain spreads into the buttock or down the leg
• You notice numbness, tingling or weakness
• It hasn't settled after a week or two

Picking up on these early usually makes them much easier to sort out.

## Post 2 — management (week of 2 November)

Looking after your back as a golfer 🏌️

Most golf-related back pain settles with the right care, and a lot of that care happens during the working week rather than on the course.

Habits that help
• Break up sitting: stand or walk for a couple of minutes every 30 to 45 minutes.
• Stretch the hip flexors: kneel on one knee, tuck the tailbone under and lean forward gently. Hold 30 seconds each side.
• Free up the upper back: lying on your side, rotate your top arm and chest open towards the floor behind you. Ten slow reps each side.
• Warm up first: a few minutes of walking, hip circles and half swings before the driver comes out.

When you're sore
• Keep moving gently. Walking usually beats lying down for days.
• Heat can ease muscle tightness in the first few days.
• Shorten the session for a while: nine holes or a small bucket instead of a full round.
• Build back up over a couple of weeks.

Common treatment approaches
• Hands-on treatment to ease tight muscles and stiff joints in the hips, upper and lower back
• Mobility and strength exercises for the hips and core
• Small changes to desk set-up and daily routine

Pain down the leg, numbness or weakness needs a proper assessment rather than playing through it.

If your back keeps complaining after golf, an osteopath can help you find out why.
