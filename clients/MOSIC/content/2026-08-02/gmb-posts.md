## GMB POST 1: ~2 August
### Angle: the problem — what's causing it, who it affects, warning signs

Common football injuries 🥅

The World Cup always sends people back to the pitch. Social leagues fill up, five-a-side courts book out, and a few weeks later the same injuries start appearing.

**Where players get hurt**

Hamstring strains: The most common football injury by a distance. The hamstring is under most strain in the moment before your foot lands at full sprint, and that's usually when it goes.

Groin and adductor strains: Caused by kicking, planting and sideways push-offs. Long hours sitting shorten the hip flexors and leave the groin doing extra work.

Ankle sprains: Winter grounds are uneven and tackles are late. The ligaments on the outside of the ankle take most of it.

Calf strains: Risk climbs noticeably after 30, often on a push-off in the second half.

**Warning signs worth respecting**

- A sharp grab in the back of the thigh, even if you can keep walking
- Tightness that returns every time you sprint
- Swelling or bruising appearing the next morning
- Ankle that feels unstable on uneven ground

Playing on through a hamstring twinge is what turns a two-week problem into a two-month one. Getting it assessed early usually shortens the whole thing.

## GMB POST 2: ~9 August
### Angle: management — general patient education, self-care, what to watch for

Getting back into football safely ⚽

Returning to a sport after years away is where most injuries happen. The gap between what you remember being able to do and what your body is currently conditioned for is where things tear.

**Before you play**

Sprint during the week: If your first flat-out effort happens in a match, your hamstrings are being tested cold. A few build-up runs at 70, 80 and 90 per cent is enough to change your risk.

Warm up for ten minutes: Easy jogging, leg swings, lunges, then gradually faster runs. Static stretching on its own doesn't prepare muscles for explosive work.

Build load gradually: Going from nothing to twice a week plus a Sunday game is a common way to end up injured. Add one session at a time.

**Between games**

Eccentric hamstring work: Two sets twice a week. The evidence for reducing strains is strong and it takes about five minutes.

Glutes and adductors: Bridges, split squats and Copenhagen planks. When the hips carry their share, the hamstrings stop overcompensating.

Warmth matters in winter: Cold muscles are less compliant. Keep a layer on until you've properly broken a sweat.

**If something does go**

Stop playing. Ice and rest in the first couple of days, then get it assessed rather than guessing at how long to wait. Returning too early is the main reason hamstring injuries come back.
