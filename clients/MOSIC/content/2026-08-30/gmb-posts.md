## Post 1 — the problem

Why shoulders flare up in spring 🤾

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body, which also makes it one of the easiest to irritate. As training picks back up after winter, shoulder pain becomes common, especially for people who spend the day at a desk.

What sets it up
• Desk posture: long hours rounded toward a screen shorten the chest and switch off the muscles between the shoulder blades.
• A quiet winter: less training means the shoulder loses strength and control without you noticing.
• A sudden ramp: returning to the gym, footy or cricket all at once asks a lot of a joint that's been idle.

Warning signs to watch
• A pinch when you lift your arm overhead
• An ache across the top of the shoulder that lingers after activity
• Pain that creeps into the neck or down the arm
• A shoulder that aches at night or when you lie on it

Most spring shoulder pain comes from the gap between a quiet winter and a busy return to sport, not one bad movement. Caught early, it's far easier to settle.

## Post 2 — management (general education)

Looking after your shoulders 💪

A few simple habits keep the shoulders moving well, whether you sit at a desk all day or you're ramping up training this spring.

Through the work day
• Reset your posture every 30 to 40 minutes: roll the shoulders back and gently squeeze the shoulder blades together.
• Set your screen at eye height so you're not craning forward.
• Stand and move on phone calls rather than hunching over the desk.

Open and strengthen
• Doorway chest stretch: forearms on the frame, lean through slowly, hold 30 seconds.
• Band pull-aparts or wall slides daily to wake up the mid-back muscles that support the shoulder.
• Add overhead and loaded work back gradually over a few weeks, not all at once.

When to get it checked
• Sharp pain, weakness, or a shoulder that wakes you at night
• Pain that isn't improving after a couple of weeks of self-care

Small, consistent habits do more for shoulder health than the occasional big stretch. If pain lingers, it's worth having it assessed.
