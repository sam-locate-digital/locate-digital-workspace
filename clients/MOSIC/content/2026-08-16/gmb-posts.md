## GMB POST 1: ~16 August
### Angle: the problem — what's causing it, who it affects, warning signs

Why running injuries spike in spring 🏃

The first mild mornings send everyone back to the paths at once. Fitness returns within a few weeks, but the tendons and muscles that absorb the pounding take much longer. That gap is where people get hurt.

**Where runners break down early in the season**

Achilles and calf strain: A winter at a desk leaves the calf short and weak, then one run asks it to load thousands of times. Common in men over 35.

Shin pain: The ache along the inside of the shin when mileage climbs too fast on hard footpaths.

Runner's knee: Dull pain around the kneecap. Sitting switches the glutes off, so the knee copes with load it shouldn't carry alone.

Plantar heel pain: Turns up in people who go from nothing to four runs a week.

**Warning signs worth respecting**

- A sharp pull in the calf or behind the ankle
- Shin ache that lingers into the next morning
- Knee pain that builds through a run
- Heel pain that's worst on the first steps of the day

Running through early-stage shin or Achilles pain is how a niggle becomes a season off. Getting it looked at early usually shortens the whole thing.

## GMB POST 2: ~23 August
### Angle: management — general patient education, self-care, what to watch for

Coming back to running the safe way 👟

Returning after a winter off is where most running injuries happen. Your fitness comes back faster than your legs do, so the trick is holding back while your tissues catch up.

**Starting out**

Run less than you want to: The first sessions should feel almost too easy. Run-walk, a few minutes running then a minute walking, is how you get to week six still running.

Add slowly: Grow your weekly distance by roughly ten per cent, not more. Add one run a week and hold it before adding another.

Watch the surface: Coming straight onto concrete is hard on shins. Mix in grass or a softer path early.

**Building resilience**

Calf raises: Straight-leg and bent-leg, a few sets twice a week. The best insurance for the lower leg.

Glute work: Bridges and single-leg exercises wake the glutes up after months of sitting, so the knees stop copping it.

Check your shoes: A few hundred kilometres flattens the cushioning even if the uppers look fine. Old runners quietly cause shin and knee pain.

**If something goes**

Back off. An ache that's still there the next morning means too much, too soon. Rest a couple of days, then get it assessed rather than guessing how long to wait. Coming back too early is the main reason these injuries return.
