Common Injuries for AFL Players 🏉

AFL's unique combination of running, jumping, tackling, and rapid direction changes creates a distinct injury profile. Recognising warning signs early prevents minor issues becoming season-ending problems.

Lower Limb Injuries:
Hamstring Strains:
The most common AFL injury. Explosive sprinting and kicking place enormous load on hamstrings. Fatigue and previous injury significantly increase risk.

Groin Strains:
Kicking and rapid direction changes stress adductor muscles. Often chronic and difficult to manage without proper treatment.

Ankle Sprains:
Landing from marks and uneven ground create frequent ankle injuries. Untreated sprains lead to chronic instability.

Knee Injuries:
ACL tears, MCL sprains, and meniscus damage from contact and pivoting require immediate assessment.

Upper Body & Trunk:
Shoulder Injuries:
Dislocations and rotator cuff damage from marking contests, tackles, and falls are extremely common.

Corked Thighs:
Direct contact creates deep muscle bruising requiring proper treatment to prevent complications.

Back Pain:
Spinal loading from running, jumping, and contact creates chronic lower back problems.

Prevention is Key:
Regular osteopathic assessment identifies weaknesses before injury strikes. Early intervention keeps you on the field longer.

Managing Workplace Stress: Your Health Matters 💼

Stress is normal when facing challenges, but chronic stress affects your health. As osteopaths, we see the physical toll daily.
Physical Signs of Stress:

-Fatigue and exhaustion
-Sleep difficulties
-Muscle tension and headaches
-Gastrointestinal upsets

Psychological Signs:
-Feeling sad or hopeless
-Worrying excessively
-Difficulty concentrating
-Feeling overwhelmed

Common Workplace Triggers:
High workload, lack of support, major changes, financial concerns, and competing priorities.

How to Manage Stress:
Self-Care Essentials:
Regular exercise (even short sessions), quality sleep, healthy eating, time with supportive people.

Workplace Strategies:
Take screen breaks, prioritise tasks, set boundaries, share workload, be kind to yourself.

Physical Relief:
Deep breathing, mindfulness exercises, and movement throughout your day reduce tension.

When to Seek Help:
If you've felt stressed, overwhelmed, or anxious most of the last two weeks, or usual strategies aren't helping, see your doctor.

The Osteopathic Connection:
We treat stress-related physical symptoms — tension headaches, neck pain, tight shoulders, back problems. Manual therapy combined with stress management creates lasting relief.

Marathon Training Without Injury - Part 2 🏃‍♂️

In Part 1, we covered training foundations. Now let's dive into recovery strategies, nutrition, and managing pain.

Recovery: The Missing Link
Sleep is Non-Negotiable:
Aim for 7-9 hours nightly. Tissue repair, muscle adaptation, and hormonal regulation happen during sleep.

Active Recovery:
Easy 20-30 minute jogs, swimming, or cycling flush metabolic waste without adding training stress.

Mobility Work:
Spend 10-15 minutes daily on tight areas — calves, quads, IT bands, hip flexors. Prevents compensation patterns causing injury.

Nutrition for Marathon Training
Fuel Your Runs:
Carbohydrates are essential for endurance training. Inadequate fuel leads to fatigue and breakdown.

Protein for Repair:
Aim for 1.2-1.6g per kg bodyweight daily for muscle rebuilding.

Anti-Inflammatory Foods:
Omega-3s, leafy greens, berries, and turmeric support recovery and reduce inflammation.

When Pain Strikes
48-Hour Rule:
If pain persists beyond two days or worsens during runs, seek assessment immediately.

Don't Self-Diagnose:
Professional diagnosis provides clarity and proper treatment plans.

Modify, Don't Stop:
Maintain fitness with cross-training while recovering from running-specific injuries.

Marathon Training Without Injury 🏃‍♂️

Training for a marathon is incredible, but up to 65% of marathon runners experience injury during training. It doesn't have to be you.

Common Training Mistakes:
Too Much, Too Soon:
Jumping from casual running to marathon training without base-building overloads tissues before they adapt.

Ignoring Strength Training:
Running alone doesn't build the muscular resilience needed for marathon training loads.

Neglecting Recovery:
Your body adapts during rest, not runs. Without recovery, fatigue accumulates and injury risk skyrockets.

Pushing Through Pain:
Persistent pain requires assessment. Ignoring it turns minor issues into major injuries.

Smart Training Principles:
Build Your Base:
Spend 8-12 weeks establishing consistent easy mileage before starting a marathon plan.

Strength Train Weekly:
Focus on glutes, hamstrings, calves, core. Single-leg exercises prevent imbalances.

Listen to Your Body:
Pain lasting beyond 48 hours needs professional assessment. Early intervention prevents major setbacks.

Vary Your Training:
Mix road, trails, and grass to reduce repetitive strain.

Progressive Loading:
Gradual increases allow tissues to adapt safely.

Get Professional Assessment:
Biomechanical screening identifies weaknesses before they cause problems.

Recovery Tips Over Easter 🐰

The Easter long weekend is here — time to rest, recharge, and enjoy family. But between travel, chocolate, and routine changes, your body might need extra care.

Common Easter Complaints:
Travel Fatigue: Long drives create neck stiffness, lower back pain, and tight shoulders.

Overindulgence: Rich food and alcohol increase inflammation and disrupt sleep quality.

Activity Changes: Sudden hiking or backyard cricket can trigger muscle soreness if unprepared.

Sleep Disruption: Different beds and late nights affect recovery.

Easter Recovery Strategies:
Stay Hydrated:
Water flushes inflammation and keeps joints lubricated. Aim for 2-3 litres daily, especially with alcohol consumption.

Movement Breaks:
Every hour of sitting requires movement. Gentle stretches and short walks prevent stiffness.

Mindful Eating:
Enjoy treats, but balance with nutritious meals. Your body recovers better with proper fuel.

Quality Sleep:
Maintain consistent sleep schedules where possible. Poor sleep amplifies pain.

Stretch Daily:
10 minutes of gentle stretching maintains mobility and prevents post-holiday stiffness.

Travel Tips:
Stop every 90 minutes on road trips. Walk, stretch your hips and back, hydrate.
