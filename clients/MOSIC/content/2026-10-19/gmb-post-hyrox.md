Training for Hyrox? Keep your body race-ready 🏋️

Melbourne race week is 9–13 December, so there are about seven weeks of training left. This is when most Hyrox injuries show up, because running volume and station work tend to ramp up at the same time.

Where it tends to go wrong
• Running: shins, Achilles and calves cop it when the kilometres climb too quickly.
• Sandbag lunges and wall balls: front-of-knee pain from high reps on tired legs.
• Sled push and pull: lower back and hamstrings, especially once form fades late in a session.
• Farmers carry: lower back, neck and forearms.

Simple ways to stay on track
• Build your running gradually rather than jumping a big chunk in one week.
• Practise the stations in race order so you know how they feel on tired legs.
• Keep one or two strength sessions a week for hips, calves and trunk.
• Plan at least one easier day each week, and protect your sleep.
• Warm up properly, especially on cold early mornings.

Signs to get it checked
• A niggle that's sharper than last week or slower to settle
• Pain that changes how you run or lunge
• Soreness that's still there the next morning

Caught early, most training injuries can be managed so you keep training in a modified way instead of stopping. Leave it until race week and you'll have fewer options.
