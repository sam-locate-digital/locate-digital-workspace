GMB POST 1: ~5 July

Lower back pain at the desk ❄️

Lower back pain is the most common complaint we see through winter. More desk hours, less movement, and cold commutes all contribute. The cause is usually mechanical.

Why sitting loads the lower back:
- Hip flexors shorten with sustained sitting and tilt the pelvis forward, increasing the lumbar curve
- Glutes and hamstrings switch off when seated, leaving the lower back to carry load they should share
- Lumbar discs need movement to stay healthy. Long periods of compression without movement take a toll
- Cold shortens the muscles around the spine, adding to what has already built up at the desk

Where the pain usually comes from:
- L4/L5 and L5/S1 joints: the lowest lumbar segments, most often restricted in desk workers
- Sacroiliac joint: often confused with lumbar disc pain because the referral patterns look similar
- Shortened hip flexors pulling the pelvis forward, which overloads the lumbar extensors over time

Pain that follows this pattern almost always has a clear mechanical cause.

GMB POST 2: ~12 July

Managing lower back pain 💪

Most people with desk-related back pain reach for anti-inflammatories and wait it out. That helps short term, but without changing the mechanical patterns behind it, the pain tends to come back.

What treatment typically involves:
- Lumbar joint mobilisation at L4/L5 and L5/S1, where restrictions are most common in desk workers
- Hip flexor work: shortened hip flexors are a primary driver of lumbar overload and respond well to manual release and stretching
- Sacroiliac joint assessment, particularly when pain sits low in the back or refers into the hip
- Thoracic mobility work, because a stiff mid-back forces the lumbar spine to compensate with more movement than it should

What helps between appointments:
- Cat-cow mobilisation: on all fours, alternate between arching and rounding the lower back. Ten reps, twice daily
- Kneeling hip flexor stretch, held for 90 seconds each side. Consistency over two to three weeks changes the resting length
- Standing breaks every 45 minutes, even briefly. Getting up and walking does more than a standing desk
- Pillow between the knees when sleeping on your side. It reduces rotational strain on the sacroiliac joint overnight

If back pain is affecting your sleep or sport, it is worth getting a proper assessment.
