# Pain between the shoulder blades: why Melbourne CBD desk workers feel it most in November

It's November in Melbourne. The Cup is run, the year-end deadlines are stacking up, and the working day on Collins Street or Flinders Lane has quietly stretched from eight hours to ten. Somewhere around 3pm, there it is again: a deep, nagging ache between the shoulder blades, usually on one side, that you can't quite reach.

For an osteopath in Melbourne CBD, this is one of the most common complaints from city professionals at this time of year. It rarely starts with an injury. Nobody remembers the moment it began. It builds over weeks of long sitting, short lunches, and stress that settles in the upper back.

Of all the back pain Melbourne desk workers bring in, this is one of the easiest to overlook, and often one of the more manageable. This kind of pain usually responds well once you understand what's driving it. It tends to be a load and movement problem, not a damage problem. Most of it comes down to how your upper back is being used for ten hours a day, and that's something you can change.

Here's what's going on, what osteopathic treatment looks at, and what you can do at your desk this week.

## Why the area between your shoulder blades takes the hit

Your upper back (the thoracic spine) is built to move. It rotates when you turn to look behind you, and it extends when you sit tall or reach overhead. At a desk, it does very little of either.

When you slump toward a laptop, the upper back rounds and the shoulder blades drift forward and apart. The muscles between them, mainly the rhomboids and the middle part of the trapezius, are then held on a stretch for hours. Muscles don't like working at that length all day. They fatigue, tighten up, and start to ache.

A few other patterns pile on:

**Head forward.** The further your head sits in front of your shoulders, the harder the upper back has to work to hold it there. A laptop on a desk almost guarantees this.

**One-sided load.** A mouse or trackpad on the right, a bag on the right shoulder for the walk from Southern Cross, a phone tucked to one ear. The same side keeps working, so the ache is often one-sided.

**Stiff rib joints.** Each rib joins the spine at small joints that move a little with every breath. Long periods of sitting still and shallow, stressed breathing can leave them stiff and sore. That's why some people feel a sharp catch on a deep breath.

**Stress.** Deadlines raise muscle tension through the neck and shoulders. If your shoulders are up near your ears by the end of a meeting, your upper back has been working the whole time.

## How osteopathy helps

Osteopathy for desk workers starts by looking at the whole working day, not just the sore spot. An osteopathic assessment works out why the area is sore, not just where. Your practitioner will ask about your work set-up, hours, training and stress, then look at how your upper back, neck, shoulders and ribs move.

Common findings include a stiff segment of the thoracic spine, rib joints that don't move well with breathing, tight muscles at the front of the chest pulling the shoulders forward, and a neck that's compensating for all of it.

Treatment is hands-on and adjusted to what's found. It may include soft tissue work to the muscles around the shoulder blades and chest, joint mobilisation to the upper back and ribs, and gentle techniques to ease the neck. The aim is to restore movement where it's been lost, so the area stops taking more than its share of the load.

## What you can do this week

You don't need an hour at the gym to make a difference. Small, regular changes through the workday usually matter more.

**Raise the screen.** If you work on a laptop, use a stand and a separate keyboard and mouse. The top of the screen should sit around eye level. This one change takes a lot of load off the upper back.

**Bring the mouse closer.** Keep it right beside the keyboard so your elbow stays near your side. Swap hands for an hour if you can manage it.

**Move every 30 to 45 minutes.** Stand up, roll your shoulders back, and walk to the kitchen or the printer. A movement break does more for an aching upper back than perfect posture held still.

**Try these three at your desk:**
- *Seated rotation:* sit tall, cross your arms over your chest and turn slowly to each side. Ten each way.
- *Chair extension:* hands behind your head, lean back gently over the top of your chair so your upper back arches. Hold for a breath, then return. Repeat five to eight times.
- *Doorway chest stretch:* forearms on a door frame at shoulder height, step through until you feel a stretch across the chest. Hold for 30 seconds.

**Breathe low.** A few slow breaths into your belly and lower ribs between meetings can ease tension in the shoulders and help the rib joints move.

**Use the walk.** If you commute into the CBD, carry your bag on alternate shoulders or switch to a backpack, and take the stairs at Parliament or Flagstaff instead of the escalator.

## The end-of-year crunch in Melbourne

For a lot of people working around Bourke Street and the wider CBD, and for those commuting in from Essendon, Carlton or Fitzroy, November and early December are the most sedentary weeks of the year. Projects need wrapping up, end-of-year reviews land, and Christmas functions start filling the calendar. Lunchtime walks disappear. Training gets skipped.

Then the holidays arrive, and many people go straight from ten-hour desk days to long drives down the coast, a week of backyard cricket, or carrying luggage and kids through the airport. A stiff, overworked upper back doesn't love that sudden change.

That's why it pays to deal with this now rather than in January. A few weeks of better habits, and treatment if you need it, can mean heading into summer moving freely instead of nursing a knot between your shoulder blades. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can I see an osteopath in Melbourne CBD without a referral?**
Yes. Osteopaths are registered primary healthcare practitioners in Australia, so you can book directly without a GP referral. You'd only need one for some funded pathways, such as a Chronic Disease Management plan through Medicare. The Bourke Street clinic is a short walk from Parliament station, and we also see patients in Essendon North and via telehealth. Bring along any scans or reports you already have, along with details of your work set-up.

**What's the difference between an osteopath and a physio for upper back pain?**
There's a lot of overlap. Both are university-trained, registered health practitioners who assess and treat musculoskeletal pain and use exercise as part of care. Osteopaths tend to use more hands-on treatment and look at how the whole body is working together, such as how your ribs, neck and shoulders affect your upper back. Physiotherapists often lean more heavily on exercise programmes. The best choice is usually a practitioner you trust who explains your problem clearly.

**How many sessions will I need for pain between the shoulder blades?**
It depends on how long the pain has been there and what's driving it. Pain that has built over a few weeks often settles with a small number of sessions alongside changes to your desk set-up and some daily exercises. Longer-standing or recurring pain may need more time, with sessions spaced further apart as things improve. Your osteopath should give you a rough plan after the first appointment and review it as you go, rather than booking you in indefinitely.

**Can pain between the shoulder blades come from the neck?**
Yes, and it's quite common. Irritated joints and tight muscles in the lower neck can refer pain into the area between the shoulder blades, so the spot that hurts isn't always the source. Stiff rib joints can also produce a sharp pain beside the spine when you breathe in. Less commonly, pain in this area can come from the heart, lungs or gallbladder, which is why pain with chest symptoms, breathlessness or nausea needs a GP or emergency check first.

**Is osteopathy for upper back pain covered by private health or WorkCover?**
Many private health extras policies include osteopathy, so you may be able to claim part of the fee on the spot with HICAPS. Check your policy for annual limits. If your pain is related to your job, such as a work set-up that has caused or aggravated it, it may be covered under a WorkSafe Victoria claim once the claim is accepted. Our reception team can explain what you'll need to bring for either option.
