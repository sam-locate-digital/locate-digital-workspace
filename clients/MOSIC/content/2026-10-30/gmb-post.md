Golf and back pain: common myths ⛳

Lower back pain after golf is common, and so is the advice that gets passed around the clubhouse. Some of it helps. Some of it keeps people sore for longer.

Myths worth rethinking
• "It's all in my swing." Technique plays a part, but stiff hips and a stiff upper back from long days at a desk often push the twisting into the lower back.
• "A sore back means something's damaged." Most golf-related back pain comes from irritated joints and overworked muscles. Scans often show disc changes in people with no pain at all.
• "Rest until it's completely gone." A day or two of taking it easy is fine. Long stretches on the couch usually leave you stiffer.
• "A few more balls at the range will loosen it up." A big session on a sore back tends to add load, not ease it.
• "Stretch your lower back and it'll come good." The lower back is often already doing too much. Freeing up the hips and upper back usually matters more.
• "Back pain just comes with golf." It's common, not inevitable. Better hip and core strength and mobility help a lot of players.

Worth taking seriously
• Pain that spreads into the buttock or down the leg
• Numbness, tingling or weakness
• Pain that wakes you at night or hasn't settled after a couple of weeks

If your back keeps flaring after a round, it's worth finding out why rather than playing through it.
