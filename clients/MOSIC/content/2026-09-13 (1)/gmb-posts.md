## Post 1 — the problem

Why footy bodies break down in finals 🏉

Finals are the most physical month of the season, and they land on a body that's been playing since autumn. For a lot of weekend players, that's when the wheels start to wobble.

What sets it up
• Accumulated load: twenty-odd weeks of training and games with not quite enough recovery in between.
• A busy September: finals are more intense, and the gap between matches can be shorter.
• Old niggles: the tight calf or grumbly back you've managed all season has had months to dig in.
• Desk life: sitting all week between games leaves the hips tight and the back stiff before you start.

Warning signs to watch
• A niggle that's sharper or slower to settle than last week
• Soreness that's still there the next morning
• A sudden loss of power or speed
• Pain that makes you change how you run or kick

Most end-of-season breakdowns come from an under-recovered body meeting the hardest month of the year, not one bad tackle. Caught early, they're far easier to manage.

## Post 2 — management (general education)

Recovering between footy games 💪

Backing up week to week in finals comes down to how well you recover between games. Most of what works is simple and free.

In the 48 hours after a game
• Sleep is the big one: aim for seven to nine hours, and protect the two nights after you play.
• Move gently the next day with a walk, easy ride or swim, rather than sitting all day.
• Get some protein and carbs in within an hour or two of the final siren.
• Keep the water up across the whole week, not just on game day.

Through the week
• Warm up properly before training, especially on cold Melbourne mornings.
• Ease off the intensity midweek if you're still sore from the weekend.
• Do a little light mobility for the hips and back if you sit at a desk all day.

Sore or hurt?
• Soreness that eases as you move is normal.
• Sharp pain, weakness, or an ache that's worse the next morning means back off and get it looked at.

Good recovery is what lets you play your best footy when it counts.
