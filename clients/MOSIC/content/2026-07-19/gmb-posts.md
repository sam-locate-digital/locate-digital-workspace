GMB POST 1: ~19 July

Melbourne Marathon prep injuries 🏃

Melbourne Marathon is 12 October. July is when training ramps up and the injuries start showing. Most are mechanical and predictable.

Common injuries in marathon training:
- IT band syndrome: lateral knee pain from hip abductor weakness and increased mileage. The knee hurts, but the hip is usually the source
- Shin splints (MTSS): inner shin soreness from too much load too fast. Often worse at the start of runs and improves after warming up, until the load gets high enough that it doesn't
- Plantar fasciitis: heel pain that's worst after rest and on first steps in the morning. Usually comes from tight calves contributing to overload through the foot
- Achilles tendinopathy: morning stiffness that eases during the day but worsens with load increases. A training load problem, not an acute injury

Why they happen:
- Mileage increases that outpace the body's adaptation rate
- Running primarily on concrete in the CBD and inner suburbs increases tibial and heel load
- Not enough hip and calf strength work alongside the running volume

These injuries don't appear from bad luck. They appear from load management.

GMB POST 2: ~26 July

Managing running injuries 🏃

Training injuries in a marathon build don't have to mean stopping. Understanding what's driving them changes how to manage them.

What assessment typically covers:
- Hip strength and pelvic control, particularly for IT band and knee pain. The cause is usually above the painful site
- Training load: how much mileage was added per week and how the body is responding to it
- Calf and Achilles length: shortened calves are a consistent contributor to plantar fasciitis and shin load
- Foot mechanics and running surface, especially for anyone running high mileage on CBD concrete

What helps in training:
- The 10% rule: increase weekly mileage by no more than 10% per week. Most training injuries appear when this is pushed
- Single-leg calf raises (3 sets of 15, twice a week) build the Achilles and plantar resilience that prevents heel and shin injuries
- Hip abductor strengthening (lateral band walks, step-downs) addresses the femoral rotation that drives IT band problems
- Mix running surfaces where possible. The Tan Track and Yarra Trail give softer footing than CBD streets

A persistent niggle in training is easier to deal with now than six weeks before race day.
