GMB POST 1: ~21 June

Winter headaches: what's really going on ❄️

Tension headaches are one of the most common complaints we see from May to August. Cold weather, longer hours at a screen, and less movement all contribute. They tend to stack.

Why winter triggers more headaches:
- Cold shortens the muscles at the base of the skull, increasing tension on nerves that refer pain into the head
- Forward head posture at a screen places roughly 20kg of load on the cervical spine at full forward tilt
- Reduced movement through the day means the neck and upper traps stay loaded without release
- Dehydration is more common in winter. People drink less water when they're not sweating

Where the pain comes from:
- Suboccipital muscles (base of skull): often the primary trigger
- Upper trapezius, which refers pain up the side of the neck and into the temple
- Sternocleidomastoid, less obvious, but can contribute to behind-eye and forehead pain
- Tight thoracic spine limiting neck movement from below

Headaches that follow this pattern usually have a postural and muscular origin, not a medical one.

GMB POST 2: ~28 June

How tension headaches are treated 💆

Most people reach for painkillers. They work short term but don't address why the headache keeps coming back. Understanding the underlying cause makes a real difference.

What treatment typically addresses:
- Cervical joint mobility: restrictions at C1/C2 are a common driver of referred head pain and often respond well to manual therapy
- Suboccipital muscle tension: the small muscles at the base of the skull tighten with sustained screen use and can be released with targeted soft tissue work
- Thoracic stiffness: a stiff mid-back forces the neck to compensate, so treating the neck alone often isn't enough
- Jaw tension: frequently contributing, especially in people who clench or grind

What helps between appointments:
- Chin tucks: pull your chin straight back (not down) to offload the base of the skull. Do 10 reps, a few times a day
- Suboccipital release: rest the base of your skull on a tennis ball while lying down, 60 seconds each side
- Screen height: raise your monitor so the top of the screen sits at eye level
- Water: drink before you feel thirsty, especially in heated offices during winter

If headaches are frequent or worsening, it's worth getting a proper assessment.
