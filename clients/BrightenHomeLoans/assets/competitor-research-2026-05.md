# Competitor research — Brighten Home Loans
## May 2026

---

## Competitive landscape — summary

All three competitors position themselves as the alternative to the Big 4 banks, but each occupies a different slice of the market: Pepper Money chases credit-impaired and self-employed borrowers across multiple loan types; RedZed is hyper-specialised in self-employed only and is essentially broker-facing; Resimac is broad but institutional in tone, leaning on its 40-year heritage. None of them clearly own the "creditworthy borrower who just doesn't fit the standard bank box" positioning — which is exactly Brighten's territory. Direct-to-consumer digital acquisition is weak across all three.

---

## Pepper Money — peppermoney.com.au

### Positioning
Broad alternative lender targeting anyone the banks have turned away — credit-impaired, self-employed, and non-standard income. Not mortgage-specific: they also do car loans, personal loans, and commercial finance.

### Key messages
- "Need really helpful loan options? That's us."
- "We deal in real life situations"
- "Flexible loan options to suit you"
- Emphasis on hardship support and human understanding

### Ads
**Google:** Active — search ads confirmed on brand terms. Copy themes likely follow the "real life" campaign angle.
**Meta:** Ran a major brand campaign in 2022 (TV, radio, outdoor, social) under the "Real Life" platform. Current Meta status — manual check required (facebook.com/ads/library — JS app, did not render).

### SEO and reviews
- **Product Review AU:** 4.4 stars, 1,552 reviews (strong volume)
- **Trustpilot AU:** Active listing, 800+ reviews
- Positive themes: "helped when nobody else would", fast approvals (2 days mentioned), hardship support praised
- Negative themes: rates consistently flagged as above market for long-term customers ("still 0.7% above market"), poor online tools ("unusable internet interface"), slow discharge process
- **MFAA 2025 Best Specialty Lender, 2024 Non-Bank of the Year** — credentials they use actively

### Social
Not fetched — check @peppermoney on Instagram/Facebook manually.

### Gaps and weaknesses
- Rates are a consistent long-term retention problem — customers refinance away once credit profile improves
- Online tools score poorly across all reviews — a structural weakness they haven't fixed
- Positioning is broad and emotionally soft ("real life") — doesn't speak specifically to creditworthy borrowers who just have complex situations
- Not a specialist mortgage lender — car and personal loans dilute the brand for home loan seekers

## RedZed — redzed.com

### Positioning
Self-employed specialist, exclusively. Founded 2006 with a single focus: Australians who run their own businesses. Broker-facing by design — redzed.com.au is consumer-facing but broker.redzed.com is the operational hub.

### Key messages
- "Backing those who back themselves"
- "We're for the self-employed"
- "A human finding a way to say yes — not a computer saying no"
- Alt-doc income verification: "different options to prove your income"
- 48-hour conditional approval, 14-day formal approval

### Ads
**Google:** Not confirmed — direct-to-consumer search presence limited (primarily broker-driven acquisition).
**Meta:** Manual check required — site blocked WebFetch, could not reach Ad Library.

### SEO and reviews
- No listing found on Product Review AU or Trustpilot — consistent with broker-only distribution model
- 24,500+ self-employed Australians helped, $10B+ in loans (from their own claims)
- 5,000+ accredited brokers nationally

### Social
LinkedIn active (company page confirmed). Consumer social presence not confirmed.

### Gaps and weaknesses
- Narrow by design — self-employed only. Has no product for salaried borrowers with complex situations
- No meaningful direct-to-consumer digital presence. All volume runs through brokers
- No reviews on consumer platforms — trust signals rely entirely on broker relationships
- "Backing those who back themselves" is a strong line but it boxes them into a single archetype

## Resimac — resimac.com.au

### Positioning
Broad non-bank lender. ASX-listed, 40 years operating, institutional heritage. Positioned as a full-service alternative across home loans, car loans, asset finance, and secured business loans. Tone is accessible but corporate.

### Key messages
- "Make it happen with Resimac"
- "40 years of helping Australians make more happen"
- "A lender that fits your life"
- "Efficient answers" and "built around you" — both vague
- Close to $50 billion in mortgage-backed securities issued since 1987 (wholesale credibility claim, not consumer-facing in feel)

### Ads
**Google:** Not confirmed from search results.
**Meta:** No campaign evidence found. Manual check required.

### SEO and reviews
- **Product Review AU:** 4.2 stars, 485 reviews (low volume for a 40-year company)
- Positive themes: individual staff praised by name, proactive outreach appreciated
- Negative themes: new person every call ("had to explain it all again"), account access issues (one customer had no account access for 6 months), rate pass-through frustration
- Online tools score: 3.1 out of 5 — lowest of any category, and lowest of the three competitors

### Social
Not confirmed. Check @resimac on Instagram.

### Gaps and weaknesses
- Review volume is low relative to their size and age — suggests weak direct relationship with borrowers (wholesale/broker-led model)
- Headline "make it happen" and "a lender that fits your life" are generic — no specific hook for any borrower type
- Digital tools are consistently rated the worst category. No evidence they're addressing it
- Communication consistency is a recurring complaint — not a boutique or hands-on lender experience
- $50B in RMBS is a wholesale/investor metric, not meaningful to a home loan customer

## Opportunities for Brighten

1. **Own "creditworthy but complex"** — Pepper Money goes after credit-impaired borrowers; RedZed only does self-employed; Resimac is generic. The borrower with a good credit history who just has a complex situation (multiple incomes, unusual property, timing challenge) is not being spoken to directly by any of these three. Brighten has the proof points and product set to own this positioning explicitly.

2. **Bridging loans — zero competition in digital** — none of the three feature bridging loans prominently in their marketing. It's the product with the least competitive noise in paid and organic search. A borrower searching "bridging loan Australia" or "buy before you sell loan" has almost no specialist lenders talking to them directly.

3. **Speed as a specific, evidenced claim** — RedZed owns "48-hour conditional approval" but only for self-employed. Pepper Money implies speed but doesn't commit to numbers. Resimac's reviews mention slow processing. If Brighten can put a specific turnaround time in its ads and landing pages, it's a differentiator nobody else is currently holding.

4. **Online experience** — all three score poorly on digital tools. Resimac is at 3.1/5. Pepper Money has reviews calling their interface "unusable". This is the category where a clean, simple application journey becomes a reason to choose Brighten over a competitor, not just a hygiene factor.

5. **Direct digital acquisition** — RedZed is broker-only. Resimac is primarily wholesale. Pepper Money has some direct presence but no clear digital acquisition strategy for home loans specifically. The direct channel is genuinely underserved by all three. Brighten's goal of reducing broker dependency is well-timed.

## Implications for campaigns

**Ad copy:** Lead with the specific borrower situation, not the lender category. "Refinancing but your bank won't move?" outperforms "flexible home loans". "Buying before you sell?" owns a whole search segment nobody is bidding on.

**Landing pages:** Every competitor avoids showing rates — Brighten doesn't need to break that convention, but should show a clear, fast path to a personalised rate. Social proof from borrowers in specific situations (refinancers, bridging loan borrowers) is more compelling than generic testimonials.

**SEO:** Bridging loan content is thin in this market. A well-structured guide — "how bridging loans work in Australia", "bridging loan vs deposit bond" — has low competition and targets a high-intent, time-pressured borrower.

**Meta/social:** Pepper Money used broad emotional campaigns ("real life"). A more targeted approach — direct response ads aimed at homeowners approaching fixed rate expiry, or those in the middle of a property transaction — is more suitable for Brighten's direct acquisition goal and hasn't been done by any of these three at scale.
