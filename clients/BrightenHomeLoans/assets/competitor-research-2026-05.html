Executive summary

The direct digital channel is wide open — and Brighten already has the product set to own it

Brighten competes in a non-bank lending market growing at 25% annually — more than six times the rate of major banks. Every competitor in this analysis is broker-dependent, with direct digital acquisition treated as secondary to broker relationships. Brighten is in the same position today, but has a stronger product set than any of them to build a direct channel around.

The most important finding: none of the three competitors offer residential bridging loans. Pepper Money actively redirects bridging enquiries to personal loans. Resimac and RedZed have no bridging product. Brighten has the product, 48-hour conditional approval, and no competition in the direct digital channel for this borrower.

There is also a significant competitive event: Pepper Money is in acquisition talks with Challenger Limited ($760M bid, February 2026). If the deal completes, Pepper comes under a retirement-focused financial services group — creating uncertainty in their mortgage strategy at exactly the time Brighten is building its direct channel.

Competitive event to monitor: Pepper Money confirmed a $760M takeover proposal from Challenger Limited in February 2026. Advanced talks, not yet complete. Under Challenger (a retirement income specialist), Pepper's mortgage focus may narrow or their broker relationships may be disrupted. Watch for deal completion.

Current weaknesses

Broker-first by design

Website and brand language leads with broker empowerment, not borrower benefit. Direct consumer acquisition is structurally underdeveloped — the same as all competitors, but a gap Locate Digital can address.

Rates not published

No rates on the website. Third-party sources show rates from 8.34% p.a. — higher than Resimac's 6.39% because Brighten's published products include specialist/alt-doc lending. Prime product rates likely competitive but not visible.

Brand awareness — 9 years vs 25–40

Brighten launched in 2017. Pepper Money has 25 years, Resimac has 40. Consumer brand recall is lower. This matters less for broker volume; it matters significantly for direct-to-consumer acquisition.

Opportunities

Five gaps Brighten can act on now

1
Publish the 48-hour approval claim immediately Already confirmed

Brighten already offers 48-hour conditional written approval — confirmed via third-party review. This is not in any current marketing. RedZed uses "48-hour conditional approval" as their headline differentiator. Pepper Money says "business days." Resimac has no speed claim. Putting this in Brighten's ad headlines and landing pages costs nothing and closes the biggest messaging gap overnight.

2
Own bridging loans in digital — entirely uncontested Uncontested

Pepper Money, RedZed, and Resimac all have zero residential bridging loan presence in the direct digital channel. Pepper actively sends bridging borrowers away to personal loans. A dedicated landing page, Google Ads campaign on "bridging loan Australia" / "buy before you sell", and a simple content guide would immediately own this space with no media competition.

3
Position explicitly for "creditworthy but complex" Positioning gap

Pepper Money is moving upmarket (prime originations up 148%) but their brand still signals credit-impaired borrowers. RedZed is self-employed only. Resimac is generic. The creditworthy borrower with a complex situation — unusual property, timing challenge, multiple income streams — has no lender speaking to them directly. Brighten's product set and criteria match this borrower exactly.

4
Move while Pepper Money is distracted Timing

The $760M Challenger acquisition bid is not yet complete. Acquisitions of this size create internal distraction, broker uncertainty, and potential strategic pivots. Brighten has a 12–18 month window to build brand awareness and direct channel volume while the market leader is occupied with a restructure. This timing opportunity will not repeat.

5
Expat and non-resident loans — zero competitor presence Uncontested

None of the three competitors offer expat or non-resident home loans. Brighten does. This is a smaller volume segment than bridging loans but has extremely high loan values (expat borrowers often purchase premium properties) and near-zero organic or paid competition. A dedicated SEO and PPC campaign would own this segment completely.

Strategic recommendation

What Locate Digital recommends

Brighten should not try to out-spend Pepper Money in the broad "alternative lender" category. Pepper has 25 years, $17.7B AUM, and 15,000 brokers. The play is to own the product verticals Pepper and others have vacated, and build a direct digital channel in the 12–18 months while Pepper is occupied with an acquisition.

Recommended positioning: "The non-bank lender for Australians with options, not problems." Pepper Money owns credit-impaired. RedZed owns self-employed. Resimac is generic. Brighten owns the creditworthy borrower who needs speed, a specific product, or criteria the Big 4 won't accommodate — without the credit-impaired association. Bridging loans, construction, and expat lending are the proof points.

Immediate actions — first 30 days

1

Add "48-hour conditional approval" to every existing ad headline and the brighten.com.au homepage. This costs nothing. Brighten already offers it. It closes the messaging gap with RedZed and surpasses Resimac immediately.

2

Build a bridging loan landing page — clear product explanation, who it's for, what the process looks like, CTA to enquire. Launch Google Ads on "bridging loan Australia", "buy before you sell", "bridging finance residential". Zero competitor noise. No media cost competition from Pepper, Resimac, or RedZed.

3

Confirm Brighten's prime home loan rates with the lending team — if competitive with Resimac's 6.39%, publish them. The entire sector hides rates; being the one lender that publishes a competitive prime rate is a direct response differentiator for Google Ads.

Actions — 3 to 6 months

4

Build SEO content for uncontested terms — "how bridging loans work in Australia", "buy before you sell: your options", "construction loan vs standard home loan", "home loans for Australian expats". All have low competition and high borrower intent. Content that Pepper, Resimac, and RedZed are not producing.

5

Launch direct response Meta campaigns targeting homeowners approaching fixed rate expiry (35–55, property owner interests) and homeowners actively transacting (buying/selling signals). None of the three competitors are running this. Test bridging loan creative separately — narrower audience but extremely high conversion intent.
