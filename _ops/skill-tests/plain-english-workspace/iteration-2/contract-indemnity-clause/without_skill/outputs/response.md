Short answer: yes, it's bad. As written, this is about as one-sided as an indemnity clause gets, and I wouldn't sign it unchanged.

## What it means in plain English

"If anything to do with the work we hire you for ends up costing us money, you pay us back. All of it, whoever's fault it was, even if it was ours. There's no limit on how much, and you're still on the hook after the contract ends."

Going through it piece by piece:

- **"indemnify and hold harmless the Client, its officers, employees and agents"**: you have to cover their losses, and you can't come after them for anything related. It also covers their staff and contractors, not just the business.
- **"any and all losses, liabilities, damages, costs... and expenses"**: every kind of loss. Nothing in the clause excludes indirect losses like lost profits or lost revenue, so those are in.
- **"legal costs on a solicitor-client basis"**: you'd pay close to their full legal bill. A court normally awards only part of it.
- **"arising out of or in connection with the Services"**: this is the dangerous bit. The loss doesn't have to be caused by you doing something wrong. It only has to be *connected* to the work you did, and courts read "in connection with" very widely.
- **"howsoever caused, including by negligence"**: this most likely includes *their* negligence as well as yours. If they make a mistake that touches your work, you still pay.
- **"save to the extent caused by the Client's wilful misconduct"**: the only way out is if they did something wrong *on purpose*. Carelessness, sloppiness or a bad decision on their part doesn't count.
- **"uncapped"**: no maximum. In theory it could be far more than you'll ever earn from them.
- **"survives termination"**: it still applies after the contract ends, for work you did while it was running.

## Why it matters for an agency like yours

Here are some realistic situations where this clause could hit you:

- They give you an image, testimonial or claim to use in an ad, it turns out to infringe someone's copyright or breach the ACL, and they get a demand letter. That's "in connection with the Services", so you cover it, even though it was their content.
- A Meta or Google ad account gets suspended during a campaign and they say they lost sales. Again, it's "in connection with", and lost profit isn't excluded.
- Their CRM leaks lead data from a form you built, and they face a Privacy Act complaint plus legal costs.
- A landing page you built goes down because of a hosting outage on their side, and they claim lost leads.

In most of these you'd have little or no liability under the ordinary law. The clause creates it.

## The insurance problem

This is the part people miss. Professional indemnity insurance generally covers what you'd be legally liable for *anyway*. It usually excludes liability you've agreed to take on under a contract beyond that. So if you sign this, there's a real chance part of any claim would land on you personally or on the business, with no insurance behind it. Check your PI policy wording, or ask your broker before you sign: "Would I be covered for a contractual indemnity like this?"

## Is it normal?

Clients, especially larger ones with in-house templates, often start with a clause like this. That doesn't make it reasonable for a small agency, and pushing back is completely normal. Most clients expect it. A fair version is usually mutual, limited to your own fault, and capped.

## What to ask for

These are the changes I'd ask for, roughly in order of importance:

1. **Limit it to losses you actually cause.** Change "arising out of or in connection with the Services, howsoever caused" to losses caused by your breach of the contract, your negligence, or your wilful misconduct.
2. **Reduce it where they contributed.** Your share should go down to the extent the loss was caused by them, including their negligence, their content, or their instructions.
3. **Put a cap on it.** The usual cap is the fees paid in the previous 12 months, or a fixed amount that sits within your insurance cover.
4. **Exclude indirect and consequential loss.** That means no lost profits, lost revenue or loss of opportunity.
5. **Make it mutual**, or at least add an indemnity from them for the material they give you (images, copy, data, claims about their product).
6. **Remove "solicitor-client basis"**, or replace it with "reasonable legal costs".

Here's a suggested replacement you could send them or hand to a lawyer:

> 14.2 The Supplier indemnifies the Client against any loss, liability, damage, cost (including reasonable legal costs) or expense suffered by the Client to the extent caused by the Supplier's breach of this Agreement, negligent act or omission, or wilful misconduct in providing the Services. The Supplier's liability under this clause is reduced proportionately to the extent the loss was caused or contributed to by the Client, its personnel, or any materials or instructions supplied by the Client.
>
> 14.3 Neither party is liable for any indirect or consequential loss, including loss of profit, revenue or opportunity.
>
> 14.4 The Supplier's total liability under or in connection with this Agreement is limited to the fees paid by the Client in the 12 months before the event giving rise to the claim.

And a short note to go with it:

> Hi [name], thanks for sending the agreement through. Mostly all good. The one thing I need to change is clause 14.2: as written it's uncapped and covers losses that aren't caused by us, which my insurance won't cover. I've suggested a standard mutual version that's limited to our own fault and capped at 12 months' fees. Happy to jump on a call if easier.

## One caveat

I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice. Indemnities and liability caps interact with state proportionate liability laws, the ACL and your insurance policy in ways that are worth getting checked. If this client is large, or the contract is worth a lot to you, it's worth paying a commercial lawyer for an hour to review the full agreement.
