Short version: Locate has earned more than $75k in a year, so you now have to charge GST, and four times a year you report to the ATO how much GST you charged and how much you paid. The clock is ticking on step 1.

## What to do, in order

**1. Get registered for GST, within 21 days (urgent)**
The 21 days runs from when you went over $75k (or when you could see you were going to), not from when you got the email. The easy way is to reply to your accountant and ask them to register you. They do this all the time.

**2. Answer the backdating question before they register you**
"EDR" is your effective date of registration, meaning the day you're treated as GST-registered. They're asking if you want that date to be earlier than today.
- **If you backdate:** you owe GST on every sale since that date, even on invoices where you didn't charge GST. That money would come out of your pocket unless you can reissue those invoices with GST added. The upside is you can claim back the GST on business purchases from that date too.
- **If you don't:** you start from the registration date and nothing before it counts.

Ask them: "What would backdating cost me versus save me, in dollars?" Have that answer before you decide.

**3. Sort out your pricing and tell your clients**
From your registration date, you add 10% GST to what you charge. On a $2,000 retainer the client pays $2,200 and $200 of that goes to the ATO. Most of your clients are businesses registered for GST, so they claim that $200 back and it usually costs them nothing. Still, let retainer clients know before the first invoice with GST on it goes out. The other option is to keep your prices the same and absorb the GST, which means you keep about 9% less.

**4. Fix your invoice template**
From the registration date, every invoice needs to be a valid "tax invoice". That means it shows:
- the words "Tax invoice"
- your business name and ABN
- the date
- what you did for them
- the GST amount, or a line saying the total includes GST
- for invoices of $1,000 or more, the client's name or ABN as well

If you use Xero or similar, switch GST on and the template will mostly take care of this.

**5. Keep receipts for anything you buy over $82.50**
To claim back the GST on business purchases (software, ads, equipment, contractors), you need a proper tax invoice from the supplier for anything over $82.50 including GST. Pull them into your accounting software or a folder as you go. The ATO expects you to keep them for 5 years.

**6. Put GST aside every time you get paid**
This is the bit that catches people out. The GST in each payment isn't your money. Move 1/11 of each payment into a separate account so you have it ready when the BAS is due.

**7. Lodge your first BAS by 28 Feb**
The BAS (Business Activity Statement) is the quarterly form where you tell the ATO:
- **1A:** the GST you charged clients
- **1B:** the GST you paid on business purchases

You pay the difference. "Q2" is October to December 2026, so if you're registered from some point in that quarter, that's your first one, due 28 February 2027. Your accountant lodges it for you.

**8. Expect PAYG instalments at some point (maybe)**
This is a separate thing from GST. PAYG instalments mean prepaying your income tax each quarter instead of getting one big bill after tax time. The ATO decides whether you're in, usually after your tax return shows enough business income. If it applies to you, the amount just shows up as an extra line on the same BAS. You don't need to do anything now, just plan for it.

## The jargon, decoded

- **Accruals basis:** GST counts in the quarter you send the invoice, even if the client hasn't paid yet. (The other way, "cash basis", counts it when the money arrives.)
- **Input tax credits:** the GST you paid on business purchases, which you claim back.
- **Registered agent:** your accountant, lodging on your behalf.

## Questions to send your accountant

1. Can you register me for GST now? (Answer their backdating question in the same email.)
2. What would backdating cost me versus save me, in dollars?
3. Why accruals and not cash basis? Businesses Locate's size can usually choose cash, which is easier on cash flow because you only pay GST on invoices that have been paid.
4. Do you expect PAYG instalments to apply to me this year, and roughly how much?

Your accountant has your actual numbers, so go with them if anything here doesn't match what they say. This should give you enough to reply to them today, though.
