What worked, what didn't

Worked

  • A fuller diary. 871 appointments held, up 19.8% on March and well clear of the January (608) and February (651) dip. Cancellations fell from 96 to 83 and no-shows from 18 to 12. Be honest about the cause: some of this is the usual recovery after summer, and we can't say how much came from marketing versus existing patients rebooking.
  • Blog content is bringing people in. Organic search was the biggest channel at 1,593 sessions. Two blog posts sit in the top five landing pages: the posterior chain post (298) and the back pain while washing dishes post (188).
  • New patients held steady at 48 despite fewer website visitors.

Didn't

  • Website traffic dipped. Sessions down 8.8% (2,821 vs 3,093) and engaged sessions down 12.5%. April is a shorter month with Easter in it, but don't put it all on that; say we'll watch whether it holds in May.
  • We can't yet show what Google Ads delivers. GA4 tracked only 2 bookings to Paid Search in April, while 103 of the 106 tracked bookings show as Direct. That points to missing tracking, not to ads doing nothing, but right now nobody can see the link between ad spend and new patients. This is Locate's to fix.
  • New patients didn't grow. 48 is the clinic's normal level. Nothing this month moved it up.

Next month

  1. Set up proper booking tracking. Install Google Tag Manager, track Cliniko bookings, phone taps and enquiry forms, and tag the Google Ads campaigns. Why: today the ads can't be judged on new patients. What they'll see: from the May report, bookings split by source, with Google Ads shown on its own.
  2. Build the TAC and scoliosis pages. Both are on the plan for May and June. Why: they target patients searching for specific funded or condition-based care, which brings new patients rather than repeat visits. What they'll see: pages live, then early organic and paid visits in the following report.
  3. Fix broken pages and keep the blog going. Audit the 404 errors and keep posting. Why: the blog is already the strongest source of new visitors, and broken pages waste them. What they'll see: the 404 list cleared and organic sessions back above March's level.

Handle carefully

  • "Is Google Ads actually working?" This is the hardest question and the honest answer is that we can't show it yet. If it comes up: "Honestly, I can't prove it either way yet, and that's on us to fix. The bookings aren't being tagged back to the ads, so they show up as direct visits. Tracking is the first job in May, and from the next report you'll see Google Ads patients on their own line."
  • Don't call April the best month of the year. The Mar + Apr deck says 871 is the highest month in the trailing 12 months, but July 2025 (894) and October 2025 (875) were higher. Say "back to last year's levels" instead.
  • Appointments by location don't add up. Bourke St 698 plus Essendon 185 is 883, not 871 held. If asked: "The location split counts slightly differently in Cliniko. I'll check it and send you the reconciled figure."
  • Practitioner numbers. The report ranks practitioners by appointments. Don't use it to comment on individuals in front of the practice manager unless the owner raises it.
