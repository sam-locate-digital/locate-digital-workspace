Usage questions

  1. "Think about the last enquiry that came through from the Facebook or Instagram ads. What happened to it, from the moment it landed?"
    Probe: Who picked it up? How long until someone called? Where did it end up?
    Listen for: a named person, a response time, and an outcome. Vague answers ("someone gets to them") usually mean leads are sitting.
  2. "Who at your end looks after the leads from the ads? Is it you, or someone on the team?"
    Probe: Are they doing anything differently with these leads compared with referrals or walk-ins?
    Listen for: whether a broker or an admin owns follow-up, and whether ad leads are treated as second-class. Could suggest another person to bring on the 90-day call.
  3. "Of the people who've come through the ads, how many have you actually sat down with?"
    Probe: Any that have gone to application yet? What were they like, first-home buyers, refinancers, upgraders?
    Listen for: his count, not ours. The type of borrower tells us whether targeting matches the business he wants.
  4. "Has anyone mentioned our social posts to you, a client, a referrer, someone on the team?"
    Probe: Has the team been sharing or commenting on them? Anything we've posted that you didn't like?
    Listen for: any sign the organic work is being noticed, and any discomfort with tone that he hasn't raised because he's been quiet.
  5. "Is there anything you were doing before we started, on social or for new clients, that's dropped off since?"
    Probe: Anything you've been meaning to tell us but haven't got round to?
    Listen for: the reason behind the quiet. It might be busy-and-fine, or it might be a niggle he's been sitting on.

Likely blockers

  • Slow or patchy follow-up on ad leads: offer a simple follow-up script and a same-day notification setup, and a 20-minute session with whoever handles leads.
  • Lead quality ("tyre kickers", wrong loan type or location): offer to tighten the form questions or targeting this month, and agree what a good lead looks like for Maxi in one sentence.
  • Access or compliance details still outstanding: if GA4, Business Manager, Google Business Profile or the credit rep number are still missing, offer to send a one-page list today with exactly what's needed and from whom, so it takes him ten minutes.
  • Not seeing reports or not reading them: offer a short monthly 15-minute walk-through instead, or a one-paragraph email summary if he prefers.
  • Content approvals slowing posts down: offer a batched monthly approval, or a list of standing topics he's happy for us to post without sign-off.

Results and proof

  • "What's different about new business now compared with before we started in September?"
  • "Roughly how many new enquiries are you getting in a month now? Before we started you mentioned about ten."
  • "Has anyone who came through the ads gone to application or settlement yet? Without names, what sort of loan was it?"
  • "How do you feel about how Maxi looks online now, compared with before?"
  • "If a broker friend asked you what working with us has been like, what would you tell them?"

Write his numbers down exactly as he says them. Don't round them, don't turn them into a percentage he didn't say, and don't offer a figure for him to agree with. Note that a 60-day window is short for settlements, so "not yet" is a normal answer.

"That's a great way to put it. Would you be comfortable with me using that as a quote, with your name and Maxi Financial? I'll send it to you to approve first."

Any quote used in marketing has to stay clear of approval or outcome promises. Keep it to his experience of working with Locate.

The referral ask

Only if the call has gone well and Jeffery is clearly happy. If adoption is low, leads are being ignored, or he's lukewarm, skip it and fix the problem first. Ask at 90 days instead.

"Glad it's working. Is there another business owner you know, maybe another broker or someone you refer with, who's trying to get more new clients online? If you're happy to, I'll send you a two-line note you can forward."

Fallback: "No pressure at all. If someone comes to mind, I'd be grateful for the intro."

Expansion signals to listen for

  • Interest in Google Ads, SEO, the website or email. Note it, don't pitch. Say you'll come back with a proposal.
  • Wanting help with document checks or other back-office work, given the loan document review work done for Maxi. Note it, don't pitch.
  • New brokers joining, a second office, or a push into a new loan type. Note it, don't pitch.
  • Staff interest in using AI day to day. Note it, don't pitch.

Handle carefully

  • Ad compliance (NCCP / ASIC): if the credit licence or credit rep number still hasn't been supplied, it matters for the ads. Raise it as a practical item, not a telling-off. Don't give legal advice. If it comes up: "That's one for your licensee or compliance adviser to confirm. Once they have, we'll make sure it's in the ads."
  • Borrower details: if he starts describing a specific client's loan, steer away from names and personal financial details. If it comes up: "No need for names, just the type of loan is plenty."
  • Staff follow-up: if leads are going cold because of one person, talk about the process, not the person. If it comes up: "Sounds like the hand-off needs tightening. Let's look at how leads get to whoever's free."
  • Missing reports or gaps on our side: if he raises that he hasn't had a report or update, own it plainly and give a date. If it comes up: "You're right, that should have come through. You'll have it by [date]."
  • Other clients: no other client's results or names.
