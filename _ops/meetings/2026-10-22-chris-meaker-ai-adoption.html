Opening

"Not sure how much you've seen of what I'm doing now, so the short version: I run Locate Digital, and the part I'm most focused on at the moment is practical AI training for brokers. You talk to more brokers than almost anyone I know, so I wanted your honest read before I get too far into it."

Must-ask questions

  1. When a broker last vented to you or one of your BDMs about their workload, what was it actually about?
    Probe: What does that look like day to day? Who does that work at a typical brokerage, the broker, a PA or a loan processor?
    Listen for: a named task (doc collection, file notes, serviceability, chasing clients), how much time it eats, and who owns it. That tells you who's in the room for the workshop.
  2. Looking across your network, what separates the brokers who grew this year from the ones who didn't?
    Probe: Is it support staff, tech, referral partners, something else? Can you think of one who changed how they work?
    Listen for: whether tech or AI comes up without you raising it. If it doesn't, AI isn't yet seen as a growth lever, and the offer may need to lead with time saved rather than "AI".
  3. Of the brokers you deal with, who's actually using AI now, and for what? Has any of it shown up in the files that reach your team?
    Probe: Anything go wrong? Notes that read like a chatbot wrote them, figures that didn't match, documents that raised a flag?
    Listen for: real examples of AI use going wrong. If lenders are already seeing problems, the policy and governance part of the offer is real, not theoretical.
  4. When brokers want to learn something new, where do they actually go? Aggregator, MFAA or FBAA sessions, BDMs, each other?
    Probe: What's the last training or PD session you saw brokers genuinely turn up for? Who paid for it? Did CPD points matter?
    Listen for: the channel to reach brokers at scale, who holds the training budget (broker, principal, aggregator), and whether CPD accreditation is a must-have before launch.
  5. Some aggregators are rolling out their own AI tools to brokers. How are brokers responding to those?
    (Press mentions Mortgage Choice giving brokers Gemini. Unconfirmed, so only name it if Chris brings it up.)
    Probe: What gets used and what gets ignored? Do they get any training with it?
    Listen for: whether aggregators are a competitor (they'll train their own) or a channel (they hand out tools and nobody shows brokers how to use them).
  6. If you were me, how would you get in front of the first 20 brokers?
    Probe: Who would you call first? Any events or groups worth turning up to?
    Listen for: names, events, aggregator contacts. This also warms him up for the ask.

If there's time

  • Who says yes to something like this at a brokerage: the principal, an ops manager, or the broker themselves?
  • Is it solo brokers or the bigger brokerages with staff who are struggling more with admin right now?
  • Has the recent fraud and accreditation news changed how lenders look at broker files, or what brokers worry about?
  • Have you seen anything from the aggregators or ASIC lately on AI use, like file notes or document checks? (Ask openly. Don't quote Report 840 as saying anything about AI.)

Test the offer

"It's a half-day workshop for a brokerage team on using AI for document checking, marketing and admin, plus a written AI use policy built around NCCP and best interests duty, and check-ins at 30, 60 and 90 days. What's your honest reaction? You won't offend me. I'd rather hear it from you than from 20 brokers who don't call back."

Then: "What would make a broker say no?" and "Who in a brokerage would actually say yes?"

Have ready, share only if asked (from the pitch deck, AUD ex GST): $997 for 1–4 staff (workshop and policy); $2,200 for 5–15 staff (adds 90-day check-ins); from $4,500 for 15+ staff (custom, multiple sessions). Deck stat: 55% of mortgage brokers already use AI daily or regularly (pitch deck; original source not named there, so say "I've seen figures around half"). The 74% / 39% figures in the deck are about financial advice practices, not brokers, so don't use them here.

Be straight that it's pre-launch: there's no broker case study yet and no CPD accreditation yet. Don't use the deck's Small Business Technology Investment Boost line. That measure looks to have closed in June 2023 (unconfirmed, check before it goes anywhere).

The ask

"This has been really useful. Could you think of two brokers in your network who'd give me 20 minutes to talk about how they handle admin? Not a pitch, just questions like today. I'll send you a two-line blurb you can forward so it's no work for you."

Fallback: "Is there one person, maybe at an aggregator or one of your state managers' best brokers, you think I should be talking to? I'm happy to reach out myself and mention you suggested it, if that's OK."

Meeting two

"Once I've spoken to those brokers, can I come back and show you what I heard, and what I've changed because of today?"
  1. What the brokers said: the two or three pains that came up most.
  2. What changed in the offer because of his feedback.
  3. His read on the best channel to reach brokers at scale (aggregator, association, PD events).
  4. His view on who'd be a good first pilot brokerage.

Handle carefully

  • Locate's marketing work for Brighten: don't mention it at all. That work is aimed at growing Brighten's direct channel and reducing its reliance on brokers, which is Chris's channel. If he asks what clients you work with, or whether you're doing anything with Brighten: "I do marketing for a few finance businesses, but I keep client work separate and don't talk about it. Today I'm really here for the broker side." Don't deny it if he asks directly. Deflect, don't lie.
  • RAM: Brighten's parent is Real Asset Management Group, which is also a Locate client. Same rule. Keep it out.
  • Brighten as a partner for the training: don't float Brighten sponsoring or co-running broker workshops. It would tangle the two pieces of work together. If he suggests it himself, thank him and say you'd want to think it through: "That's generous. Let me get the offer right first and come back to you on that."
  • Brighten's direct-to-consumer plans, products or numbers: don't raise them, and don't react knowingly if he does. Anything you know comes from client work.
