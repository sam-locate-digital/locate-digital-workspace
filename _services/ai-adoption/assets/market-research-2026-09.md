# Market research — AI Adoption (Locate Digital new service)

**Prepared by:** Locate Digital
**Date:** September 2026
**Subject:** AI training/upskilling services for Australian businesses (staff-facing tool adoption — not systems implementation)
**Geography:** Australia-wide
**Purpose:** shape positioning and pricing for a planned launch

---

## 1. Executive snapshot

- **AU corporate training market:** USD 3.5B (2023) → USD 5.4B by 2028; global AI-powered corporate training segment USD 6.27B (2025) → USD 18.19B by 2031
- **69%** of Australian employees now use AI regularly at work (up from 40% in July 2024) — but only **22%** have received any formal training
- **67%** of SMEs cite lack of in-house expertise, not cost or tool access, as the main barrier to structured AI adoption
- **72%** of Australian SMEs have no AI training plan at all, despite 60%+ of their staff already using AI tools unsupervised

**The opportunity:** demand for AI tools has outrun training everywhere, and it's an expertise gap, not a budget gap. Nobody in the market found during this research is selling structured, affordable, ongoing AI training built specifically for small local service businesses — the gap is between $200–500 generic self-serve courses and $5,000+ enterprise workshops that require an in-house L&D contact to organise.

## 3. Trends shaping the industry

**1. Tool adoption has decisively outrun training.** Regular AI use at work has grown from 40% (Jul 2024) to 69% (Jan 2026), while only 22% of employees have received any formal training (Plain English Foundation / National AI Centre data). *Implication: the sales pitch isn't "should you adopt AI" — most target customers already have staff using it. It's "your team is already doing this without a plan."*

**2. Tool adoption ≠ workflow-integrated adoption.** 38% of Australian SMEs use AI tools in some form, but only 15–20% have workflow-integrated AI systems (FlowWorks). *Implication: a one-off workshop doesn't close this gap — it needs an ongoing or staged program that moves a business from ad hoc tool use to embedded workflows.*

**3. A sharp generational trust divide inside the workforce.** 31% of employees aged 18–34 trust AI to help inform decisions, versus 4% of those over 55 (Amplitude, cited via SmallBizAI). *Implication: training content needs a confidence-building track for older staff and owner-operators, separate from the mechanics-of-the-tool track younger staff mostly need.*

**4. Government money is flowing toward AI adoption, but not toward Locate's typical client.** The AI Adopt Program funds AI Adopt Centres and $50,000–$250,000 grants, but eligibility is scoped to SMEs engaged in *international or interstate trade* — most local service businesses (a plumber, a clinic, a beauty salon) won't qualify. *Implication: don't lead with "grant-funded," since most prospects are ineligible for the flagship program — but the broader Small Business Technology Investment Boost (20% bonus tax deduction, uncapped eligibility up to $100k spend) is a usable, honest talking point for almost any client.*

**5. Regulatory obligations are about to make "we already use AI" a compliance question, not just a productivity one.** From 10 December 2026, Privacy Act changes require businesses to be able to explain AI-influenced decisions to employees and customers; the National AI Centre's AI6 guidance (Oct 2025) is the de facto national standard in the absence of a standalone AI Act. *Implication: a short "AI governance basics" module gives the offer a risk-management hook, not just a productivity one — useful against buyers who don't care about efficiency gains but do care about liability.*

## 4. Regulatory & structural factors

Australia has no standalone AI Act as of this research (Sep 2026); the government uses existing technology-neutral law plus the National AI Centre's voluntary AI6 guidance (published Oct 2025). Two changes matter directly for a training-service pitch:

- **Privacy Act — automated decision-making disclosure**, effective 10 December 2026: businesses must be able to explain AI-influenced decisions affecting customers or employees.
- **Workplace guidance pending**: SafeWork NSW is expected to release AI-in-the-workplace guidance; businesses are advised to review WHS risk assessments where AI tools are in use.
- **Accountability doesn't transfer to the AI vendor** — if a third-party tool produces a discriminatory or unfair outcome, liability stays with the business using it. This is a legitimate reason for even AI-sceptical owners to want staff properly trained rather than freelancing with tools unsupervised.

This is a live regulatory conversation (July 2026 saw an announcement to legislate "Australian Standards for AI" and stand up a new Office of AI) — treat anything cited here as current as of the research date and check for updates before quoting it in client-facing pitch material more than a few months old.

## 5. Target audience & customer segments

Sector-level adoption figures vary by survey methodology — two independent 2026 sources gave different sector rankings, both are shown rather than reconciled into one number:

|---|---|---|

| Finance & insurance | 64% | — |
| Business/professional services | 61% | ~45% |
| Retail & hospitality | — | ~25% |
| Healthcare | — | ~22% |
| Trades & construction | — | ~18% |
| Transport | 21% | — |

By business size: large enterprises (200+ staff) 80%+ adoption, mid-market (50–199) ~60%, small (5–19) ~38%, micro (1–4) ~33%.

**Segment A — "Using it, untrained."** Staff already using ChatGPT/Copilot/Claude ad hoc with zero formal training (the 60–69% using it vs. 22% trained gap). This is Locate Digital's existing client base almost exactly — local service businesses already in the 5–50 staff range. Trigger: risk of inconsistent quality/inputs plus wasted upside, not FOMO. Objection: "we're already using it, why pay for training" — answer with the workflow-integration gap (2 above).

**Segment B — "Haven't started, don't know where."** The 33–38% of small/micro businesses with no AI use at all, disproportionately in trades, construction, and healthcare (lowest adoption sectors, 18–22%). Trigger: peer FOMO — SMEs using AI grow 2.8x faster (MYOB) and see 25–35% productivity gains, bigger than the 15–20% enterprises see. Objection: "we don't have the data/tech for this" — this is a stated myth in the research (many wrongly believe they need large organised datasets); addressing it directly is a differentiator.

**Segment C — "Compliance-anxious leadership."** Owner-operators and managers who are cautious about AI (65% of non-adopters cite trust/control concerns, not cost) and are more receptive to a governance/risk framing than a productivity framing. Trigger: the December 2026 Privacy Act change and general liability exposure. Objection: fear of losing control — meet with the AI6 guidance framework and a "human stays in the loop" training design.

## 6. Demand signals & seasonality

- Regular AI use at work grew from 40% to 69% between July 2024 and January 2026 — a steep, sustained 18-month growth curve, not a spike.
- AI as Australians' primary way of looking things up online roughly doubled year-over-year (5% → 12%); one source cites Claude usage growing 64x in 18 months. These are general-population search-behaviour signals, not training-specific demand, but they corroborate the same underlying adoption curve.
- No AI-training-specific seasonality data was found. General corporate training industry patterns (new calendar year planning in Jan–Feb, EOFY budget spend in June) are a reasonable planning assumption but **this is inferred, not sourced** — don't present it as confirmed in client-facing material without a dedicated check closer to launch.

**Government-adjacent cost offsets** (not direct discounts on a private training provider's fee, but relevant to the pitch): Small Business Technology Investment Boost — 20% bonus tax deduction on eligible spend up to $100k (broadly available); AI Adopt Program grants $50k–250k (restricted to trade-exposed SMEs, not most local service businesses); free National AI Centre toolkits and advisory (no direct funding).

**Whitespace:** almost every priced example above is either a quote-only enterprise engagement (needs an L&D contact and a budget approval process most 5–50 staff businesses don't have) or a generic $200–500 self-serve course with no Australian small-business context and no follow-up support. There is a clear gap for a fixed-price, sub-enterprise package (roughly $1,000–$3,000 per business, not per seat) aimed at a 5–20 person team.

## 9. Strategic implications

1. **Lead with "already using it, unsupervised," not "should you adopt AI."** The target segment (Locate's existing service-business client base) is already past the adoption question — 60–69% of staff use AI tools with no training. The pitch is risk/consistency and untapped upside, not FOMO.
2. **Design the offer as staged/ongoing, not a single workshop.** The 38%-tool-use vs 15–20%-workflow-integration gap is the real opportunity; a one-off session doesn't close it. A short initial workshop plus a follow-up check-in cadence (30/60/90 day) matches how the gap actually closes.
3. **Price inside the whitespace: roughly $1,000–$3,000 per business (flat, not per-seat), for teams of 5–20.** This undercuts every quote-only enterprise competitor found and outperforms generic $200–500 self-serve courses on relevance and support, without competing on the $5k+ enterprise tier that needs an L&D buyer Locate's clients don't have.
4. **Prioritise trades/construction and healthcare as beachhead sectors.** They show the lowest confirmed adoption (18–22%) despite clear, findable use cases (scheduling, admin, client/patient communications), and no competitor found is targeting them specifically — professional services and finance are already the default market for the enterprise-oriented trainers.
5. **Add a short AI governance/compliance module as a differentiator, not the headline.** The December 2026 Privacy Act change and the "accountability doesn't transfer to the vendor" principle give a legitimate risk-management reason to buy, useful specifically against the 65%-of-non-adopters who cite trust/control rather than cost as their objection.
6. **Mention the Technology Investment Boost (20% bonus deduction), not the AI Adopt Program grant, in sales material.** The Boost is broadly available to the target segment; the flagship AI Adopt Program grant mostly isn't (trade-exposure requirement) — leading with a grant most prospects can't get would undercut credibility.
