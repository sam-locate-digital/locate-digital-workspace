Section 3

Trends shaping the industry

1

Tool adoption has decisively outrun training. Regular AI use at work grew from 40% (Jul 2024) to 69% (Jan 2026); only 22% of employees have had any formal training.

What this means: lead with "your team is already doing this without a plan," not "should you adopt AI."
2

Tool adoption ≠ workflow-integrated adoption. 38% of SMEs use AI tools; only 15–20% have workflow-integrated systems.

What this means: a one-off workshop won't close this gap — the offer needs a staged or ongoing structure.
3

A sharp generational trust divide. 31% of 18–34 year olds trust AI to inform decisions, versus 4% of those over 55.

What this means: content needs a confidence-building track for older staff/owners, separate from the tool-mechanics track.
4

Government funding skews away from Locate's typical client. The AI Adopt Program's $50k–250k grants require international/interstate trade exposure — most local service businesses won't qualify.

What this means: don't lead with "grant-funded." The broader 20% Technology Investment Boost deduction is the honest, usable talking point instead.
5

Regulation is turning "we already use AI" into a compliance question. From 10 Dec 2026, Privacy Act changes require businesses to explain AI-influenced decisions to staff and customers.

What this means: a short governance module gives the offer a risk-management hook against buyers who don't respond to a pure productivity pitch.
Section 4

Regulatory & structural factors

No standalone AI Act exists in Australia as of this research; government relies on existing law plus the National AI Centre's voluntary AI6 guidance (Oct 2025).

  • Privacy Act — automated decision-making disclosure from 10 December 2026: businesses must be able to explain AI-influenced decisions affecting employees or customers.
  • Workplace guidance pending: SafeWork NSW expected to release AI-in-the-workplace guidance; WHS risk assessments should be reviewed where AI is in use.
  • Accountability stays with the business, not the AI vendor, if a third-party tool produces a discriminatory or unfair outcome.
Note: this is a live regulatory area — a July 2026 announcement flagged plans to legislate "Australian Standards for AI." Re-check before quoting any of this in pitch material more than a few months old.

Section 6

Demand signals & seasonality

  • Regular AI use at work grew 40% → 69% over 18 months (Jul 2024–Jan 2026) — a sustained curve, not a spike.
  • AI as the primary way Australians look things up online roughly doubled year-over-year (5% → 12%); one source cites Claude usage growing 64x in 18 months — general-population signals that corroborate the same adoption curve.
Gap: no AI-training-specific seasonality data was found. Standard training-industry patterns (Jan–Feb planning, June EOFY spend) are a reasonable planning assumption but are inferred, not sourced — confirm closer to launch before using in client-facing material.

Section 9

Strategic implications

Lead with "already using it, unsupervised," not "should you adopt AI." Locate's client base is past the adoption question — the pitch is risk and untapped upside.

Design the offer as staged/ongoing, not a single workshop — a 30/60/90-day check-in cadence matches how the tool-use → workflow-integration gap actually closes.

Price inside the whitespace: roughly $1,000–$3,000 flat per business (not per seat) for teams of 5–20, undercutting every quote-only competitor without competing on the enterprise tier.

Prioritise trades/construction and healthcare as beachhead sectors — lowest confirmed adoption (18–22%), clear use cases, and no competitor found targeting them.

Add a governance/compliance module as a differentiator, not the headline — useful specifically against the trust/control objection, not the productivity pitch.

Mention the Technology Investment Boost, not the AI Adopt Program grant, in sales material — the Boost is broadly available; the grant mostly isn't for this segment.

Next step

Run competitor-research on Nexacu, Elevate Corporate Training, and Xelleron once the offer and pricing are closer to final — the three closest in positioning — to pressure-test messaging and confirm none has moved into the small-service-business segment since this research.
