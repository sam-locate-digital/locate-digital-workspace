## How to use this

### Pick how you'll record

| | Loom | iPhone + OBS, then `/edit-tutorial` |
|---|---|---|
| Retakes | One take. A big mistake means starting again | Fluff a line, pause, say it again. The edit cuts the mistakes |
| What members see | The raw screen, with your face in a small bubble | Zooms on what you click, Locate title and chapter cards, labels for tools, captions and a chapter list |
| Your time after recording | None | None. You drop the files in a folder, I edit, you watch and approve |
| Best for | Getting a video live today | The versions you keep |

**My recommendation: iPhone + OBS, edited with `/edit-tutorial`.** The Claude app's text is small and hard to read on a phone without zooms. You also don't have to get a 10-minute video right in one take. Circle takes the MP4 upload directly, plus the captions file. Setup is in `_brand/Tutorials/filming-guide.md`.

If you'd rather use Loom, the scripts work the same way. Keep the face bubble on, and keep going when you trip over a word.

### How the scripts are written

- **FACE** lines are written word for word. Read them a couple of times, then say them to camera in your own words. They don't need to be exact.
- **SCREEN** parts are steps, with *what to say* in italics. Talk it through naturally as you click. Don't read.
- **(pause)** means stop for a second after a click so the edit can zoom in.
- Everything you say follows the module text, so the video and the written lesson match.

### The made-up broker you'll play

Use the same one in every video, so the examples join up. Say once near the start of each screen video: *"I'm using a made-up practice for this, so nothing you see is real client information."*

> **Alex Morgan, Demo Home Loans, Geelong.** A solo broker with one admin assistant. Mostly first-home buyers and young families, plus some refinancers. Warm, plain English, short sentences, Australian spelling. Explains jargon in brackets. Signs off "Cheers, Alex". Never states a rate, lender policy or approval as fact. Never promises an approval. Emails under 150 words, with one next step in bold and documents as a bullet list. Clients upload documents through a secure link.

When Claude asks you questions during a video (L03-1, L04-1, L05-1), answer as Alex, using the answer sheets in those sections.

## One-off prep (about an hour, before the first recording)

1. **Make a clean demo Claude account.** Your own account shows your real chats, Projects and memory in the sidebar, and members see everything on screen.
   - Use a new email address that only receives the sign-in codes, for example a new alias on your domain. Then the inbox you show (or pause on) has nothing else in it.
   - It needs a paid plan for the folder, Projects, skills and routines videos. One month of Pro on the demo account, cancelled after the last recording, covers all of them. That's your call: it's the only cost in this pack.
   - L01-1 shows signing up, so you create this account **on camera** in L01-1. Don't make it beforehand.
2. **Make the practice folder** on the desktop of the PC you record on, called `Claude practice`. Every file must be made up: no real client, not even redacted.
   - `Bank statement - Client A.pdf` and `Payslip - Client A.pdf`. Made-up, but with a fake name, address and account number visible (L06-1 redacts them).
   - `Follow-up email 1.docx` and `Follow-up email 2.docx`. Two of "Alex's" past emails, with no names.
   - `Example emails.docx`. Three of Alex's best emails (for L04-1).
   - `File note examples.docx`. Two of Alex's file notes (for L05-1).
   - `Recent social post.docx`, and `Jobs that eat my week.docx` (a list of three).

   I can make all of these for you.
3. **Keep a Notepad file of everything you'll paste.** Every prompt in this pack goes in one Notepad file, so you copy and paste on camera instead of typing long prompts.
4. **Tidy the screen** (from the filming guide):
   - Do Not Disturb on, every other app closed, desktop icons hidden (right-click the desktop → View → untick Show desktop icons).
   - Use a separate Chrome profile for the demo account, with no bookmarks bar and no other tabs.
   - In the Claude app, Ctrl and + zooms in a step or two so text is readable on a phone. Ctrl and 0 resets it.
5. **Click through each video once off camera, on the day.** Anthropic changes the app often. If a button has moved or has a different name, say what you actually see on camera and write it down. The module text can then be updated to match.
6. **Record in this order:** L01-1 (it creates the account), L02-1, L03-1, L04-1, L05-1, L05-2, L06-1, then **L00-1 last**. The welcome is easier to say once you've made the rest.

## L01-1 · Setting up Claude from scratch

**Module 01, chapters 1–5 · 8–10 minutes · face intro and outro, then screen**

**Prep**
- Have the new demo email address ready (one-off prep, step 1). **Don't** create the Claude account yet.
- The `Claude practice` folder on the desktop with the dummy files.
- In Notepad, ready to copy: the first-task prompt (below).
- Check off camera whether Virtual Machine Platform is already ticked on your PC (Start → "Turn Windows features on or off"). **Don't untick it.** If it's already on, you show it already ticked.
- If it isn't on, ticking it needs a restart. Stop recording, restart, then start a new recording that opens Task Manager.

**Pause the recording (or the edit cuts it) whenever:**
- You open the email with the sign-in code.
- You type card details on the Billing page. Resume once you're back in the app.

**In the edit:** a chapter card for each of the five steps, labels for claude.ai and claude.com/download, the install wait sped up, and zooms on every setting.

**Script**

**FACE, intro**
> In this video we'll set Claude up from scratch, the safe way. By the end you'll have a paid account on your work email, the privacy setting right, the desktop app installed, a practice folder, and your first real task done.
>
> I'm using a brand-new account and a made-up practice folder, so nothing you see is real client information. Pause the video whenever you need to catch up.

**FACE: "First, let's check your computer can run it."**

**SCREEN, 1. Check your computer**
- Press **Windows + I**. Settings opens. Click **System**, scroll down, click **About**. (pause)
- Point at **Installed RAM**. *"You want 8 gigabytes or more. Mine says [read it out]."*
- Scroll to **Windows specifications**. *"You need Windows 10, version 2004 or later, or Windows 11. And not S mode."*
- *"On a Mac it's the Apple menu, then About This Mac. Those steps are written out in the module."*
- *"If this is a work laptop, you may need IT to approve the install. There's a note you can send them in chapter 6. Send it today, because approvals can take a day or two."*

**FACE: "Next, your account. You need a paid plan, on your work email."**

**SCREEN, 2. Set up your account**
- *"Quick check first. If your practice is on Claude Team, don't do this part. Look for an invite email from Anthropic instead, and check your junk folder."*
- In the browser, go to **claude.ai**. Click **Sign up**. Enter the demo email. *"There's no password. Claude emails you a sign-in code or link."*
- (Pause recording, get the code, resume.) Finish any welcome questions with Alex's details.
- Click **your initials, bottom left** → **Settings** → **Billing**. (pause) *"Choose Pro and pay by card. You can move the practice to Team later."*
- (Pause recording while you enter the card. Resume back in the app.)

**FACE: "Before you type anything about your business, check how your chats are used."**

**SCREEN, 3. Privacy setting**
- **Your initials** → **Settings** → **Privacy**. (pause)
- Point at the setting about using your chats to help improve Claude. *"Set this to match your practice's AI use policy. If you don't have one yet, switch it off for now. Module 06 helps you write one."*
- Switch it off. (pause)
- *"On a Team plan, your administrator may control this for everyone. If it's greyed out, that's why, and that's a good sign. Someone's made a decision for the practice."*

**FACE: "Now the desktop app. The browser's fine for quick questions, but the app is what lets Claude work with files on your computer. That's where most of the time savings are."**

**SCREEN, 4. Install the desktop app**
- Go to **claude.com/download**. Point at the Windows and macOS buttons. (pause) *"Only ever download it from claude.com or claude.ai. Ignore look-alike apps."*
- Click **Windows**. Open the file from Downloads. If Windows asks to allow changes, click **Yes**. (The wait gets sped up in the edit.)
- Open Claude from the Start menu and sign in with the same account. (Pause for the code again.)
- Right-click the Claude icon in the taskbar → **Pin to taskbar**.
- A 10-second look around: *"Message box. Model menu. The sidebar with your chats and Projects. Your initials for settings. We'll tour all of this properly in Module 02."*
- *"One more step, on Windows only. For file work, Claude needs a Windows feature called Virtual Machine Platform."*
- Start → type **Turn Windows features on or off** → open it. Scroll to **Virtual Machine Platform**. (pause) *"If it's ticked, you're done. If not, tick it, click OK and restart when it asks."*
- Press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc**. In Task Manager: **Performance** → **CPU**. Point at **Virtualization: Enabled**. (pause) *"If it says Disabled, IT needs to switch it on. The note in chapter 6 covers that."*

**FACE: "Last step, and the good one. Your practice folder and a first real task."**

**SCREEN, 5. Practice folder and first task**
- Open the `Claude practice` folder on the desktop.
  - *"I've made a folder on my desktop called Claude practice, with some dummy files in it. A bank statement and a payslip with the details blacked out. Two follow-up emails with the names taken out. A recent social post. And a list of three jobs that eat my week."*
  - *"Nothing in here should identify a real client. Until your practice has an AI policy, practise on dummy or de-identified files only."*
- In the Claude app, click **New chat**. Click the add button by the message box → add a folder → choose **Claude practice**. (pause) If Windows asks, click **Allow**.
- Paste the prompt and press Enter:

  > I'm a mortgage broker and I've just started using Claude. Look at the files in my Claude practice folder and suggest three ways you could save me time each week. For each one, tell me roughly how long it might save and what I'd need to give you. Start with the quickest win.

- Let it answer (the edit speeds up the wait). Read the three suggestions out briefly. (pause)
- Type: **Let's do number 1. Draft it now.** Show the result. Say in one honest sentence whether it's usable and what you'd change.

**FACE, outro**
> That's you set up. Your practice task: do one real task from Claude's three suggestions. Then post in the Community space what it suggested, which one you tried and how long it took.
>
> If anything didn't work, chapter 6 has the common hiccups and the note for your IT person. See you in Module 02.

**Mac version (optional).** The media list asks for one if you can. If you have a Mac, record only steps 1 and 4 again on it, as a short second video:
- About This Mac (this also gives you S01-2).
- Open the `.dmg`, drag Claude to Applications, choose Open if the Mac asks.
- Right-click the Dock icon → Options → Keep in Dock.

There's no Virtual Machine Platform step on a Mac. If you don't have one, the written Mac steps in the module are enough.

## L03-1 · Weak prompt vs five-part brief, side by side

**Module 03, chapters 1–3 · 6–8 minutes · face intro and outro, then screen**

**Prep.** In Notepad, ready to copy: the five-part brief with Geelong filled in and `Follow-up email 1` pasted underneath it, plus the three follow-ups below.

**Answer sheet** (Claude may ask questions first, because the brief tells it to):
- Names: "Use Client A and Client B."
- Secure link: "Just say 'the secure link I sent you'."
- Anything else: answer as Alex, in one line.

**In the edit:** the five parts labelled 1–5 as you point at them, and a points card ("Context, Task, Example, Format, Review") at the end.

**Script**

**FACE, intro**
> Most people type a short request, get a bland answer back, and decide AI isn't much use. That's not Claude being bad at the job. It's Claude filling the gaps with guesses. In this video I'll show you the same job done two ways.

**SCREEN, 1. The weak prompt**
- **New chat.** Type **Write a follow-up email to a client.** and press Enter.
- Read the first couple of lines. (pause)
  - *"It's fine, but it's generic. It doesn't know who I am, who the client is, what happened in the meeting, what I want them to do next, or how I write. So it guessed."*
  - *"It's like saying 'write a follow-up email' to a new staff member and walking off."*

**SCREEN, 2. The five-part brief**
- **New chat** (a fresh one). Paste the brief, but **don't press Enter yet**:

  > I'm a mortgage broker in Geelong. The client is a first-home buyer couple I met yesterday; they're nervous about the process and have two kids. Write a follow-up email thanking them for their time and asking for the documents we discussed: two recent payslips each, three months of bank statements and photo ID. Match the tone of the email below. Under 150 words, warm, no jargon, documents as a bullet list, one clear next step (upload to the secure link by Friday). Ask me anything you need before you start.
  >
  > [Follow-up email 1, pasted]

- Run the mouse slowly over each part as you name it. (pause) on each:
  - *"One, context: who I am and who the client is."*
  - *"Two, the task: one clear job."*
  - *"Three, an example: an email I was happy with, with the names taken out."*
  - *"Four, the format: length, tone and structure."*
  - *"Five, review: ask me anything you need first."*
  - *"You don't need all five every time. Context and an example make the biggest difference."*
- Press Enter. If Claude asks questions, answer from the answer sheet.
- Show the draft. (pause) *"Now compare. This one I'd send after a quick read."* Then say what's actually better on screen: the tone, the document list, the one next step.

**SCREEN, 3. Follow-ups**
- *"The first answer is a draft from a junior. Useful, not finished. The follow-ups are where it gets good. And be specific. 'Make it better' gets you vague fixes."*
- Send these one at a time, and say what changed after each:
  1. **Write it like I'd say it on the phone.**
  2. **Mention that they're worried about how long approval takes.**
  3. **Check this against what I told you. What did you assume that I didn't say?**
- After the third: *"That last one's worth using any time you're not sure a draft is right."*

**FACE, outro**
> So: context, task, example, format, review. Then push it with specific follow-ups.
>
> Your practice task: pick three real jobs from this week. Write a five-part brief for each, with anything that identifies a client taken out. Push each one with at least two follow-ups, and save your final briefs in a document called My prompts. Share your best one in the Community space.
>
> And if writing all that every time sounds like a lot, Module 04 fixes it. You'll save most of the brief once, so day to day you only type the task.

## L05-1 · Building a file note skill

**Module 05, chapters 2–3 · 8–10 minutes · face intro and outro, then screen**

**Prep**
- `File note examples.docx`: two of Alex's file notes. Paste both into the build prompt in Notepad.
- The test jottings (below) in Notepad.
- Your one piece of feedback, decided in advance: **"Always end with a Next steps section: who does what, by when."**

**Answer sheet (as Alex)**
- **Structure:**
  - Meeting details (date, who attended, in person or phone)
  - Client goals in their own words
  - Situation (employment, income, assets, debts, dependants)
  - What they want from the loan
  - Concerns they raised
  - Options discussed
  - Documents outstanding
  - Next steps
- **Must always include:** the client's goals and any concerns they raised, and the documents still needed.
- **Avoid:** client names (use Client A and Client B), writing an option as if it's the final recommendation, and stating rates or lender policy as fact.
- **Length:** "One page. Headings and short bullet points."

**In the edit:** a chapter card for build, test and improve, and a split frame showing the rough jottings next to the finished note.

**Script**

**FACE, intro**
> A skill is a saved playbook for one job you do the same way every time. Teach Claude once, and it does it your way from then on, in any chat. In this video we'll build one for file notes, test it on rough notes from a meeting, and then improve it.

**SCREEN, 1. Switch skills on**
- **Your initials** → **Settings** → find the skills option (usually under Capabilities). (pause) Make sure it's on.
- *"On a Team plan, your administrator may need to switch this on first."*

**SCREEN, 2. Build it**
- **New chat.** Paste the build prompt with your two examples:

  > I want to build a skill for writing file notes after client meetings at my mortgage broking practice. Here are two examples of good file notes [the two examples]. Ask me questions first about the structure, what must always be included and what to avoid. Then build the skill so you write them this way every time I give you meeting notes.

- Scroll briefly over the examples. *"Two of my notes, with the client details taken out."*

## L06-1 · Client data and AI: what's safe

**Module 06, chapters 1–3 and 5 · 6–8 minutes · mostly face to camera, plus one screen demo**

**Prep**
- `Bank statement - Client A.pdf`: completely made up, with a fake name, address, BSB and account number in the header (and the footer, if it has one).
- **A real redaction tool.** Adobe Acrobat Reader (the free one) can't redact. You need Acrobat Pro (there's a free trial) or another PDF editor with a Redact tool. Check before the day. If you have neither, show the module's fallback instead: copy just the transactions into a blank document.
- The face pieces are longer here. Say them in blocks, the same way as L00-1.

**In the edit:**
- A points card for the four upload levels.
- A before-and-after split of the statement.
- The decision grid as a full card, with each of the three tasks dropping into its box as you say it.

**Script**

**FACE, block 1: obligations**
> Before you use Claude on anything to do with a client, this is the module to get right.
>
> Your obligations don't change because a tool drafted the work. Under the NCCP, Best Interest Duty and responsible lending stay with you. The FBAA advises members never to put client or company personal data into public AI tools. And your privacy obligations to clients still apply.
>
> That doesn't mean you can't use AI for client work. It means you need three things: the right plan, the right habits, and a written policy everyone follows.

**FACE, block 2: the four levels**
> Think about uploads in four levels.
>
> One: personal or free accounts. Treat them like any consumer app. Dummy files only. No client documents, ever.
>
> Two: business plans, so Team or Enterprise. Commercial terms apply, and by default your inputs aren't used to train models. That's the minimum bar for de-identified client documents, once your policy allows it.
>
> Three: de-identify first, even on a business plan. Make that your habit for every upload.
>
> Four: if you need more, Enterprise has extra data agreements. Ask Anthropic, and don't assume they're included.
>
> And until your practice has a written policy: dummy or de-identified files only, and hold off connecting your inbox, calendar or CRM.

**FACE: "Here's how to de-identify a document properly."**

**SCREEN, de-identify a bank statement**
- Open the made-up statement. *"This statement is completely made up."*
- Point at what goes and what stays. (pause) on each:
  - *"Remove the name, the address, and the account and BSB numbers."*
  - *"Keep the transactions, the amounts and the dates. For a statement check, that's all Claude needs. It needs the transactions, not the client's name."*
- Choose the **Redact** tool. *"Use the redact tool, not a black highlighter. Highlighting can often be removed. Redaction actually deletes the text."*
- Mark the name, address and account numbers on every page, including the header and footer. Apply the redaction.
- Save as a new file: **Statement - Client A - redacted.pdf**.
- Open the new file and try to select the redacted text. (pause) *"If you can copy it, it isn't properly redacted."*
- *"No redaction tool? Copy just the transactions into a blank document instead. Claude only needs the rows."*

**FACE, block 3: the decision grid**
> Two questions place any task. How often do we do it, and what does a mistake cost? Let's run three real broker jobs through it.
>
> Drafting this week's social posts. Done often, and low cost if it's a bit off. So that one you can automate, and spot-check it.
>
> Checking a bank statement before lodgement. Done often, but a mistake is expensive. So AI drafts and a person signs off. This box is where the biggest time savings are, so don't let the word high-stakes scare you into leaving it all manual.
>
> A hardship conversation with a client. That needs judgement about a person. Keep it human. AI can help you prepare your notes beforehand, and nothing more.
>
> And a quick test for anything else: if you'd be uncomfortable telling the client, or ASIC, that AI did it, don't let AI do it.

**FACE, outro**
> One more thing. This is general guidance, not legal or compliance advice. Run your policy past your aggregator or your compliance adviser before you rely on it.
>
> Your practice task: check which Claude plan your practice is on, de-identify one sample document and test the redaction, and place your five tasks from Module 05 on the grid. Then draft your AI use policy with the prompt in chapter 6, and send it to whoever handles your compliance.
