# VSL script: AI integrations and training

For the video at the top of locatedigital.io/ai-training. Sam on camera, with screen recordings cut in.

- **Length:** about 3 minutes (roughly 470 spoken words at a calm pace). The section timings below are a guide. If it runs long, cut section 6 down to its last two sentences.
- **Matches the page:** same headline promise, the same three role examples as the "What it looks like in your week" demo, the same four steps, and the same next step (the 3 quick questions under the video).
- **Numbers:** the only statistic is the "around 70% of finance professionals" line already used on the site. No other figures, results or client counts.

---

## Shoot script

### 1. Hook (0:00 to 0:12)

**Say:**
If your team is using AI, it's probably happening in a browser tab. Someone copies something in, copies the answer out, and nobody's quite sure what's allowed.

For a finance firm, that's a risk as much as a missed opportunity.

**On screen:** Sam to camera, tight framing. Cut to a short screen recording of text being pasted into a chat window and copied back out.

### 2. Who I am (0:12 to 0:25)

**Say:**
I'm Sam, and I run Locate Digital. We work only with Australian financial services firms: brokers, advisers, accountants. I've spent most of my career in financial services, so I know what your team's week looks like.

**On screen:** Lower third: "Sam [surname] · Locate Digital". Optional quick cut of Sam working at a laptop.

### 3. The problem (0:25 to 0:55)

**Say:**
Around 70 per cent of finance professionals spend three or more hours a day on file work. Notes, follow-ups, document checks, proposals.

A lot of that is exactly the work AI does well. But only if it's set up inside the tools your team already uses, and only if each person is shown how to use it on their own work.

Buying licences doesn't do that. Neither does a one-hour webinar on prompts.

**On screen:** Text card: "Around 70% of finance professionals spend 3+ hours a day on file work". Then Sam to camera for the last two lines.

### 4. What it looks like (0:55 to 1:35)

**Say:**
This is what it looks like when it's set up properly.

A principal finishes a client meeting, and Claude turns the notes into a proposal summary, in the firm's own template.

Someone in client services asks for a follow-up email, and gets a warm draft that lists exactly what the client still needs to send.

The admin team checks a new client file against the checklist, and Claude tells them what's missing and drafts the request.

In every case, a person reads it, edits it and decides what goes out. AI prepares the work. Your people make the decisions.

**On screen:** Three short screen recordings, one per example, each with a small label: "Principal", "Client-facing", "Admin". Use made-up client names only (the page uses "Harbour Dental" and "Priya and Tom"). Never show real client data. End on Sam to camera for "AI prepares the work."

### 5. How it works (1:35 to 2:10)

**Say:**
We do it in four steps.

First, we look at how your team works today: your tools, where client data lives, and which tasks take the most time.

Then we set Claude up inside your email, documents and calendar, with your templates and your tone.

Then we train each role on their own work. Principals, client-facing staff and admin each get sessions built around what they actually do.

And then we stay with you. Monthly office hours, refreshers as the tools change, and a monthly report on hours saved and how much the team is using it.

**On screen:** Simple four-step graphic matching the page: Assess, Build, Train, Monitor. Highlight each step as Sam says it. A photo or clip of a training session over "we train each role".

### 6. Safety (2:10 to 2:28)

**Say:**
You also get a one-page AI use policy, so everyone knows which tools are approved and what information can go into them. Access is set at the firm level, and client data stays in Australian regions wherever the platform allows.

No one on your team needs to be technical. If they can write an email, they can use what we set up.

**On screen:** Sam to camera. Optional: a clean mock-up of a one-page policy document, with a heading only (no real policy text needed).

### 7. Next step (2:28 to 2:45)

**Say:**
If your team is curious about AI but unsure where to start, this is for you.

Right under this video there are three quick questions. It takes less than a minute. Answer them, and we'll come back to you within one business day to set up a short, private call about where AI could save your firm the most time.

**On screen:** Sam to camera, then gestures down. End card: "Answer 3 quick questions below" with a down arrow. Hold for two seconds of silence before the video ends.

Option B's last line is a general claim. Keep it only if you're comfortable saying it without a figure.

## Recording notes

- **The first 10 seconds matter most.** Say the hook straight to camera, with no logo intro or music sting before it.
- **Setting:** your office or a quiet meeting room, natural light, muted background. Your existing photos (`_brand/photos/sam-office-portrait.png`, `sam-working-laptop.png`) set the look.
- **Captions:** burn them into the video. Plenty of people watch with the sound off.
- **Screen recordings:** you can record the demo on the landing page, or real Claude with made-up client details. Never use real client names or documents.
- **Cover image:** export a frame of you mid-sentence and set it as `data-poster` on the page, so the cover isn't the stock team photo.
- **Hosting:** Wistia or Vimeo, then paste the link into `data-embed` on the page (see the comment marked `VSL` in `_brand/site/ai-training.html`).
- **Before you say "we'll come back within one business day":** make sure that's true for the volume ads might bring.
